All Conditions Precedent Are Satisfied And The Buyer Has Exercised The Call. Completion Is Expected Mid-September, Inside The Third Quarter — Which Means CICT’s Next Published Leverage Figure, Struck At 30th September, Will Sit After Both The Paragon Purchase And The Asia Square Sale, And The Interval Between Them Is Never Reported

Illustration: PropertyAtlas. All conditions precedent under the put and call option agreement for the sale of a 100 per cent interest in Asia Square Tower 2 have been satisfied and the purchaser has exercised the call option, with completion expected in mid-September 2026. Source: CapitaLand Integrated Commercial Trust Management Limited, Exercise Of Call Option In Relation To The Sale Of 100% Interest In Asia Square Tower 2, 2nd September 2026.

CapitaLand Integrated Commercial Trust said on 2nd September 2026 that all conditions precedent under the put and call option agreement for the sale of its 100 per cent interest in Asia Square Tower 2 have been satisfied, and that the purchaser has exercised the call option. Completion is expected in mid-September 2026. The announcement runs to a single operative paragraph on page 1. It restates no price, discloses no proceeds figure, and does not say what the conditions were.

On its face there is very little here. What the document actually supplies is a date, and the date is worth more than it looks.

From a half-year to a fortnight

Until today the completion guidance was a six-month range. The divestment announcement of 20th April 2026 put completion of both the Paragon acquisition and the Asia Square Tower 2 sale in the second half of 2026. The 1H 2026 results of 13th August repeated it: the tower was expected to complete in the second half at net sales proceeds of approximately S$2.45 billion, on a 3.0 per cent post-tax exit yield.

Mid-September is a fortnight. More to the point, mid-September is inside the third quarter, and the third quarter has a reporting date attached to it.

The bridge was the leg that got used

The 20th April funding structure had three legs: a private placement of at least S$600 million, the net proceeds of the Asia Square Tower 2 sale, and a debt top-up. It also carried a contingency, stated plainly in the same materials — if the Paragon completion ran ahead of Asia Square Tower 2, a bridging loan would step in, to be repaid when the tower closed.

That is exactly what happened. The placement closed first, on 29th April, at 326,087,000 units at S$2.30 for gross proceeds of S$750.0 million. Paragon completed on 1st July 2026, one day after the half-year balance-sheet date. Asia Square Tower 2 has not completed yet. The contingency leg has been carrying the gap for two months, and today’s announcement is the first document to put a date on its repayment.

The peak sits between two reporting dates

CICT discloses aggregate leverage every quarter. The published series runs 38.6 per cent at 31st December 2025, 38.5 per cent at 31st March 2026 and 37.4 per cent at 30th June 2026. The next figure will be struck at 30th September 2026.

The 37.4 per cent print deserves its context restored, and the manager supplies that context itself: the ratio takes into account the effect of temporary loan repayments using the placement proceeds. Of the S$750.0 million raised, S$195.0 million sat at 30th June as a deposit on Paragon within other non-current assets, and the remaining S$555.0 million had been used to repay borrowings on a short-term basis before being redeployed on 1st July. The half-year balance-sheet date fell inside the window in which the equity had been raised and the asset had not yet been bought.

What follows is a PropertyAtlas estimate built from disclosed figures. It is not a manager pro-forma, and the manager has published none for these dates.

Total borrowings including CICT’s proportionate share of joint ventures were S$10.7 billion at 30th June, which at 37.4 per cent implies a deposited property base of roughly S$28.6 billion. Paragon needed about S$3.65 billion of funding on completion once the S$195.0 million deposit is taken out, effectively all of it debt, since the equity had been raised and consumed in April. Applying the approximately S$2.45 billion of net Asia Square proceeds to borrowings returns the ratio to roughly 40 per cent.

The arithmetic is unremarkable. The calendar is the point. The peak opened on 1st July, the day after a reporting date, and — if completion lands where the manager expects — closes in mid-September, a fortnight before the next one. About eleven weeks at the top of the range, and no published balance-sheet date falls inside it. The 30th September position will be struck after both legs have settled.

That is not a criticism. Nothing here is concealed: the sequence was disclosed in April, the bridging contingency was disclosed in April, and the manager flagged the temporary loan repayment in its own notes. It is simply worth saying out loud that 37.4 per cent describes a moment the trust had already left, and that the moment it moved into will not appear anywhere in the reported series.

The residual risk runs the other way. If completion slips past 30th September, the third-quarter figure carries the peak instead. Today’s announcement is what makes that unlikely — with the conditions precedent satisfied and the call exercised, what remains is settlement mechanics rather than approvals.

The S$222.3 million mark loses its conditionality

CICT’s 1H 2026 total return rose 74.2 per cent, and S$222.3 million of that was a net change in the fair value of investment properties attributed in the financial statements to the valuation uplift of Asia Square Tower 2. The portfolio statement confirms it arithmetically.

S$2,476.0 million is not an appraiser’s opinion of value. It is the agreed property value at which CICT’s wholly owned sub-trust contracted on 20th April to sell the tower to IOI Marina View Pte. Ltd. The revaluation was a mark to a signed price, and the manager noted that the 30th June carrying amounts rest on an internal assessment made in consultation with external valuers rather than a fresh external valuation.

A mark to a signed price still carries whatever conditionality the contract carries. Today removes it. The conditions precedent are satisfied and the buyer has exercised; the gain booked in the first half now has a settlement date behind it rather than a condition list.

A detail on the face of the document

The announcement records that the Purchase Conditions are deemed to have been entered into between the purchaser and the CCT Trustee — not CICT’s trustee. Asia Square Tower 2 still sits inside the sub-trust structure inherited from CapitaLand Commercial Trust, six years after the merger that created CICT. The tower has been held since the 2017 CapitaLand Commercial Trust era, and it will leave through the same vehicle it arrived in.

What is not disclosed

Three things, and the omissions are ordinary rather than pointed.

The conditions precedent are not identified. The document confirms they have been satisfied without saying what they were. Note that the unitholder approval sought in 2026 attached to the Paragon acquisition, an interested-person transaction under the SGX Listing Manual; the Asia Square Tower 2 sale to a third-party purchaser did not require it.

The consideration is not restated. The announcement neither confirms nor revises the S$2,476.0 million agreed property value. Every price figure in this article is a 20th April number carried forward.

The net proceeds figure is not updated either. The approximately S$2.45 billion is guidance given in April and repeated in August, struck before whatever completion adjustments apply.

What to watch

Two things. Completion itself, expected mid-September, which converts a mark-to-contract into cash and takes gearing back down. And the third-quarter business update, which will be the first period to carry Paragon’s income, the first to show leverage after both completions, and the document in which the approximately eleven weeks between them do not appear.
