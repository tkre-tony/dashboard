Centurion Corporation's Profit From Core Business Operations Rose 34.2 Per Cent To S$87.7 Million In The First Half Of 2026, While The Portion Attributable To Its Own Shareholders Fell 15.6 Per Cent — Non-Controlling Interests Took Half The Group's Profit

Centurion Corporation's core profit rose 34.2 per cent. The share of it belonging to Centurion's own shareholders fell 15.6 per cent. Between those two numbers sits Centurion Accommodation REIT — consolidated in full, owned 38.25 per cent, and the payer of…

By PropertyAtlas·15 August 2026·13 min read
Photo: Westlite Mandai, Singapore, owned by Centurion Accommodation REIT. The new block obtained Temporary Occupation Permit in January 2026 and its FEDA licence in March 2026; a further 1,980 beds of Mandai Expanded Capacity obtained a FEDA licence in May 2026, taking total capacity to c.9,986 beds. Consolidation of Westlite Mandai contributed S$22.9 million of the S$30.6 million rise in Centurion's Singapore worker accommodation revenue in the first half of 2026. Photo: Centurion Corporation

Centurion Corporation reported revenue of S$184.9 million for the six months to 30th June 2026, up 31.4 per cent, and profit from core business operations of S$87.7 million, up 34.2 per cent. Core profit attributable to equity holders fell 15.6 per cent, to S$48.8 million. The group got bigger and its shareholders got smaller, in the same half, from the same portfolio.

The reconciling item is Centurion Accommodation REIT, listed on the SGX Mainboard on 25th September 2025 and a subsidiary of the group. Centurion holds 38.25 per cent of its units and consolidates 100 per cent of its revenue, its assets and its borrowings. Everything below the operating lines is then shared with the other 61.75 per cent.

Non-controlling interests took S$26.6 million of the S$53.1 million of total profit — 50.1 per cent, against 11.0 per cent a year earlier. On the core measure the shift is just as blunt: equity holders’ share of core profit fell from 88.4 per cent to 55.6 per cent, a transfer of 32.8 percentage points in a single half.

The same reordering shows up in the cash. Dividends paid to non-controlling interests were S$17,016,000. Dividends paid to Centurion’s own shareholders were S$16,816,000. The minorities drew more cash out of the group than the shareholders did. On the balance sheet, non-controlling interests are now 46.7 per cent of total equity, against 43.3 per cent at 31st December 2025 and 6.8 per cent at 30th June 2025.

None of this is concealed — the press release states plainly that the fall reflects a larger non-controlling interest share following the CAREIT listing and the distribution in specie completed on 2nd June 2026. But the headline chosen was the 34 per cent rise, and the 34 per cent rise is the number that belongs least to the people reading it.

One qualification belongs alongside that. The fall in reported profit is not all attribution. Share of results from associated companies swung S$31.9 million, from a S$27.8 million profit to a S$4.2 million loss, and the prior-year figure included S$28.4 million from Westlite Mandai as an associate, of which S$22.7 million was fair value gain. Mandai is now a consolidated subsidiary, so that line could not repeat. The current-period loss also carries the cost of leaving the United States, where the two remaining assets held through the US fund were divested in March 2026. This is why the core measure is the fairer comparison of the two halves — and why what happens to core after attribution is the number that matters.

The Stamp Duty That Core Profit Does Not See

Reported net profit after tax fell 36.0 per cent to S$53.1 million, driven by a net fair value loss on investment properties of S$32,756,000. Note 6(n) splits it: S$19,031,000 against investment properties and S$13,725,000 against right-of-use assets classified as investment properties.

The press release explains that the reported fair value loss includes S$19.1 million of stamp duties paid by CAREIT to acquire EPIISOD Macquarie Park, recorded as a fair value loss on investment properties. Set the two figures side by side: a S$19.1 million duty and a S$19,031,000 fair value loss on investment properties. Within S$0.1 million of each other — which means the underlying revaluation of the property portfolio in the half was approximately nil. The entire investment-property fair value loss is transaction tax.

The segment note points the same way from a different direction. Of the S$32,756,000 loss, S$19,401,000 falls in student accommodation — the segment EPIISOD Macquarie Park sits in — against S$12,901,000 in worker accommodation and S$454,000 in others. A S$19.1 million duty inside a S$19.4 million segment loss leaves roughly S$0.3 million of everything else. The balance sheet review completes the mechanism: other non-current assets fell S$16.2 million, mainly from capitalising the stamp duty into investment properties, where it was then written off through fair value.

That matters because the non-IFRS core measure adds back all fair value movement without distinguishing what caused it. A cash payment to a state revenue office, made once and never recovered, is removed from “core business operations” purely because of where the accounting put it.

PropertyAtlas recomputation, treating the duty as the cost it is: core profit becomes S$68.6 million against S$65.4 million, a rise of 5.0 per cent rather than 34.2 per cent. Applying Centurion’s 38.25 per cent economic share of a duty paid by CAREIT, core profit attributable to equity holders becomes S$41.5 million against S$57.8 million, a fall of 28.2 per cent rather than 15.6 per cent. The direction of travel does not change. The magnitude does.

The Carrying Values Are Six Months Old

Investment properties closed the half at S$3,145,589,000, up S$462.0 million. Note 6(n) records that the group carried out an internal assessment as at 30th June 2026, in consultation with the independent valuers who had performed the 31st December 2025 valuations, and found the key inputs substantially unchanged.

There was no external valuation at the half year. The S$3.15 billion book, the 24 per cent net gearing computed against it and the S$1.40 net asset value per share all rest on appraisals dated 31st December 2025. This is the second time in ten days PropertyAtlas has recorded that finding across this corporate family: CAREIT’s own 1H 2026 results, published on 5th August 2026, disclosed the same absence at the REIT level. Sponsor and REIT are consistent with one another. Neither has been tested by an outside party since the year end.

Consolidated Gearing Overstates The Parent By Roughly Half

Borrowings rose 52.5 per cent to S$1,075.8 million and cash fell 19.1 per cent to S$301.7 million. Net debt therefore rose 132.9 per cent, to S$774.2 million. Net gearing recomputes to 23.60 per cent, from 11.55 per cent; the group states 24 per cent from 12 per cent.

The results presentation then does something unusually useful: it splits the balance sheet between CAREIT and the rest of the group. The split ties exactly to the financial statements.

CAREIT carries 61.1 per cent of the group’s borrowings and 7.3 per cent of its cash — 82.1 per cent of consolidated net debt. Centurion owns 38.25 per cent of it. On a look-through basis the parent’s economic net debt is S$381.7 million, or 49.3 per cent of the consolidated figure. The 24 per cent net gearing headline is arithmetically correct and economically about twice the parent’s own exposure.

The security position tightened alongside. Secured borrowings are 86.6 per cent of the total, from 81.9 per cent at the year end, and investment properties with a carrying value of S$2,878,766,000 — 91.5 per cent of the book — are pledged. The presentation states interest coverage of 5.6 times without defining the basis; the closest reconstruction from disclosed absolutes is earnings before interest, tax and depreciation over finance expenses, at 5.49 times.

Singapore Carries The Revenue, Malaysia Carries The Beds

Worker accommodation revenue rose 31.8 per cent to S$143.1 million and student accommodation 31.4 per cent to S$40.6 million. Underneath the segment totals the geography is lopsided.

Malaysia holds 44.7 per cent of the group’s worker-accommodation beds and produces 8.7 per cent of its worker-accommodation revenue. A Singapore bed earns roughly 6.9 times what a Malaysian bed earns. That gap is structural — it reflects regulated dormitory economics in one market and a far cheaper labour-housing market in the other — but it means the Malaysian bed stack, which occupancy commentary dwells on at length, moves the group’s earnings very little either way.

Singapore revenue rose 30.9 per cent to S$129.8 million while Singapore occupancy fell from 99 per cent to 94 per cent, because bed count rose faster than utilisation. Of the S$30.6 million increase, the group attributes S$4.5 million to Westlite Toh Guan and S$22.9 million to Westlite Mandai — S$27.4 million, or 89.5 per cent of the increase, from two assets. Malaysian occupancy fell ten percentage points to 73 per cent on foreign-worker quota caps.

The Second Quarter, On Its Own

Centurion published a voluntary business update for the first quarter on 13th May 2026. Set against the half-year statements it isolates the three months to 30th June, which neither release reports directly. Both endpoints are disclosed absolutes, so the subtraction is exact; the two periods are not equally scoped, the first being a voluntary update and the second a full condensed statement, so the middle column below is a PropertyAtlas calculation rather than a reported figure.

Growth accelerated through the half, and the acceleration is not evenly spread. Australia went from 107.0 per cent growth in the first quarter to 117.7 per cent in the second, Singapore from 28.6 to 33.0 per cent, and Malaysia from 30.1 to 32.1 per cent. The United Kingdom went the other way — from 4.9 per cent in the first quarter to 1.0 per cent in the second, on revenue of S$11.6 million against S$11.5 million a year earlier. The one market the group describes as delivering sustained high occupancy is also the one that stopped growing in the second quarter, and it is the market where the group itself expects occupancy to soften in the second half.

The capacity roll splits the same way. Beds run 79,387 at 31st December 2025, c.81,388 at 31st March and c.85,528 at 30th June: 2,001 added in the first quarter and 4,140 in the second, summing to the c.6,141 the group reports for the half. Two-thirds of the half’s bed growth arrived in the final three months and has not yet earned a full quarter of revenue.

Which makes the guidance worth reading twice. The group guides second-half revenue of approximately S$190 million, about 22 per cent growth. Twice the second quarter is S$191.0 million. The guidance sits marginally below the rate the business was already running at in June. The 22 per cent is measured against a weaker second half of 2025, not against sequential momentum. On its own numbers, and with two-thirds of the half’s new beds only just opened, Centurion is guiding the second half to hold its June run rate rather than build on it.

The quarterly split also reopens occupancy, which the half-year averages flatten.

Singapore’s half-year average of 94 per cent sits below its own first quarter of 95 per cent, which places the second quarter below the first: occupancy was still falling in June as 4,140 new beds arrived. Malaysia’s half matches its first quarter exactly, so the ten-point year-on-year fall was taken in full in the first three months and has since held. In fairness to the group’s own guidance, it discloses a post-period reading that points the other way: committed occupancy at Westlite Toh Guan and Westlite Mandai stood at 99 per cent and 87 per cent as at 31st July 2026.

The Lease Renewals Nobody Headlined

Singapore capacity of c.43,938 beds is six Purpose-Built Dormitories with c.34,642 beds, four Quick Build Dormitories with c.7,256 beds, and c.2,040 beds managed for third-party owners. The four QBDs are held on short JTC leases rather than long tenures. In June 2026 the group secured a three-year extension on Westlite Tuas Avenue 2 (c.1,224 beds) with options for a further 3.5 years. It is described as the first of four.

The other three are still open. Westlite Kranji Way (c.1,300 beds) runs 6.3 years from 2020. Westlite Jalan Tukang (c.4,104 beds) runs 5.9 years from 2021. Westlite Tuas South Boulevard (c.628 beds) runs 6.3 years from 2021. All are described as in discussion. That is 6,032 beds — 13.7 per cent of Singapore capacity and 7.1 per cent of the group — on leases at or near expiry with no renewal announced.

Separately, the 1,980 beds of Mandai Expanded Capacity are approved for retention only until 31st December 2030. The FEDA licence arrived in May 2026; the sunset is already written.

The Pipeline Is Essentially One Site

The group has c.9,770 beds in development for delivery between 2027 and 2029, excluding pending tenders and applications. Every one of them is named in the release, and they reconcile to the total exactly.

Kranji Close is 71.6 per cent of the entire pipeline. Portfolio capacity is guided from c.85,528 beds today to c.87,249 by 2027; almost all of the growth beyond that sits in one tender outcome that has to be built and licensed by the third quarter of 2028. A portfolio described as diversified across five countries and four segments is, on its own forecast, becoming more Singaporean and more dependent on a single site delivering on schedule.

It arrives into company. The government released dormitory land sites for tender during the half with a combined capacity of more than 40,200 beds, for completion between 2027 and 2030 — nearly six times the Kranji Close development, landing in the same window, while operators are simultaneously retrofitting to Interim Dormitory Standards. Centurion has also tendered for Lok Yang Way and is awaiting the outcome. The tightening bed supply that supports today’s Singapore pricing is being addressed by the same authority that sets the standards driving it.

Where The Documents Disagree With Each Other

Five inconsistencies inside the same four-document release, none material to the result, all worth knowing about:

The condensed cash flow statement reports net cash provided by financing activities of S$270,124,000; the review commentary at section 8(d) says S$270.7 million. The thirteen constituent lines sum to S$270,124,000. Centurion’s stake in CAREIT is given as 38.2 per cent in the press release appendix and 38.25 per cent in the financial statements and the presentation. Interest coverage of 5.6 times is stated without a basis and does not reproduce. Net asset value per share is compared against 30th June 2025 in the presentation (S$1.44) and against 31st December 2025 in the financial statements (S$1.47). And occupancy in Hong Kong SAR worker accommodation and Xiamen is compared to 1Q 2026 while every other market is compared to 1H 2025. The first-quarter update supplies the missing bases. Hong Kong worker accommodation reads 62 per cent in 4Q 2025, 68 per cent in 1Q 2026 and 70 per cent in the half — a genuine ramp on any base. Xiamen reads 90 per cent in 4Q 2025, 83 per cent in 1Q 2026 on Chinese New Year seasonality, and 85 per cent in the half. Presented as up from 83 per cent it improves; measured against the fourth quarter it is still five points down. The comparison chosen is against the seasonal trough.

What The Shareholder Actually Got

Net asset value per share fell to S$1.3990 from S$1.4660, down 6.71 cents. In the same half the company distributed 13.00 cents — a 2.0-cent final dividend for 2025 and 11.0 cents in specie, being one CAREIT unit for every ten ordinary shares, which implies S$1.10 per unit. Underlying net asset value accretion was therefore 6.29 cents per share. The balance sheet did grow; it was simply handed out faster.

Basic earnings per share fell to 3.15 cents from 8.79 cents, and on the core measure to 5.81 cents from 6.88 cents, on a share count unchanged at 840,778,624. The interim dividend is held at 2.0 cents, with books closing at 5:00 pm on 9th September 2026 and payment on 30th September 2026. Guidance for the second half is revenue of approximately S$190 million, around 22 per cent growth.

One last line, from the related-party note. Key management personnel compensation rose 36.7 per cent to S$6,754,000, and total compensation to directors rose 37.4 per cent to S$4,238,000. Both tracked the 34 per cent rise in core profit. Neither tracked the 15.6 per cent fall in the portion of it that belongs to shareholders.
