GuocoLand's Property Development Segment Swung To A S$101.7 Million Operating Loss In FY2026 And Property Investment Carried The Group — While A Valuation Notice Filed The Same Day Valued Guoco Tower, Guoco Midtown And 20 Collyer Quay At S$5.84 Billion, Or S$4.59 Billion After Minority Interests

GuocoLand filed its full-year results and a Notice of Valuation of Real Assets on the same day. Read separately, the results are a soft year at a Singapore developer. Read together with the annual report, the three documents produce a concentration figure…

By PropertyAtlas·28 August 2026·7 min read
Photo: Guoco Tower at Wallich Street, with the Sofitel Singapore City Centre in the foreground. The office and retail component alone was valued at S$2,853.0 million at 30th June 2026, of which GuocoLand's effective interest is 80 per cent. Credit: GuocoLand.

GuocoLand reported revenue of S$1.43 billion for the financial year ended 30th June 2026, down 25 per cent, and profit attributable to equity holders of S$95.2 million, down 11 per cent. On the same day it filed a Notice of Valuation of Real Assets giving, building by building, what its investment properties were worth at the balance date.

The results tell one story. The valuation notice, read against the segment note and last year’s annual report, tells a more precise one.

One engine, not two

Group Chief Executive Officer Cheng Hsing Yao described the year as one in which the group’s twin engines continued to perform well. The segment note shows one engine running and the other reversing.

The three lines sum to S$125.019 million exactly. Property Development, which produced S$98.1 million of operating profit a year earlier, produced an operating loss of S$101.7 million. Property Investment produced S$215.5 million on revenue of S$292.5 million — a 73.7 per cent operating margin — and is the only reason there was a group operating profit at all. The characterisation is not wrong about the investment engine. It is generous about the development one.

Three buildings, and what they are worth to shareholders

The valuation notice, all four valuations dated 30th June 2026, puts 20 Collyer Quay at S$726.6 million on a CBRE valuation, Guoco Tower’s office and retail component at S$2,853.0 million on a CBRE valuation, and Guoco Midtown’s office and retail component at S$2,262.0 million on a Savills valuation. Guoco Changfeng City’s South and North Towers in Shanghai, including office, basement retail and carpark, were valued at RMB 2,934.0 million by Savills in Guangzhou.

The three Singapore assets come to S$5,841.6 million. Against investment properties carried at S$6,864.9 million and total group assets of S$11,375.4 million, that looks like more than half the balance sheet sitting in three addresses.

It is not, and the annual report is where that becomes clear. GuocoLand’s effective interest is 80 per cent of Guoco Tower and 70 per cent of Guoco Midtown. The valuation notice states property values on a 100 per cent basis.

On an attributable basis the three assets are worth S$4,592.4 million — 40.4 per cent of total group assets, and 104.1 per cent of the S$4.41 billion of equity attributable to ordinary shareholders. The share of three buildings that belongs to GuocoLand’s shareholders is worth slightly more than the entire book equity of the company they own.

Singapore rental income was S$253.9 million of the S$292.5 million investment segment, 86.8 per cent, which is the source of the release’s 87 per cent. Against S$5,841.6 million of valuations that is a gross revenue yield of 4.35 per cent, consistent with the 3.3 to 4.5 per cent Singapore commercial capitalisation rate range the group disclosed a year ago.

China: revenue up 23 per cent, written down S$207.2 million

The single largest item in the year is an allowance for foreseeable losses of S$207.2 million against development properties in Chongqing, taken after a review of estimated net realisable value.

Two points of precision. The release states S$207.2 million; the income statement line reads S$209.769 million, which note 6.2 separates into S$207.2 million and a further S$2.6 million. And the prior year figure was S$81.8 million, so the charge has grown roughly two and a half times.

The uncomfortable pairing is with revenue. China development revenue rose to S$234.9 million from S$190.8 million, up 23.1 per cent, in the same year as the write-down. The group sold more in China and marked the remainder down, which is what a decision to clear inventory at prevailing prices looks like in the accounts.

It was also visible in advance. The FY2025 annual report disclosed the capitalisation rate range used to value China commercial property widening at the top end to 3.8 to 4.8 per cent, from 3.8 to 4.5 per cent the year before, while the Singapore range held flat at 3.3 to 4.5 per cent. The softening that produced this charge was in GuocoLand’s own valuation inputs a full year before the charge landed.

Singapore development revenue fell 47.5 per cent, and that is the least alarming number here

Singapore development revenue was S$670.1 million against S$1,275.3 million, down 47.5 per cent. The group attributes this to progressive revenue recognition on projects still early in construction, and to Springleaf Residence and Faber Residence being equity-accounted joint ventures whose revenue does not appear on the consolidated line at all.

That explanation holds against the disclosures. Proportionate revenue from equity-accounted Singapore projects was S$391.0 million against S$211.0 million, and share of profit of associates and joint ventures was S$32.4 million, a turnaround from a loss. The revenue has not vanished; it has moved off the consolidated line and forward in time, and the group says the projects are substantially sold. Development properties on the balance sheet fell 31.5 per cent to S$1,605.3 million, consistent with a book being worked through rather than replenished.

A dividend larger than earnings

The board proposed a first and final dividend of 8.0 Singapore cents per share, raised from 7.0 cents, payable on 18th November 2026 subject to approval at the annual general meeting. Basic earnings per share were 7.36 cents, down from 8.43 cents. The payout is 108.7 per cent of basic earnings per share, and the dividend was increased 14 per cent in a year when earnings per share fell 13 per cent.

The case for it is on the record and is reasonable. Underlying operating profit — which includes share of results of associates and joint ventures and excludes the allowance — was S$367.2 million against S$376.5 million, down 2 per cent. The Chongqing charge is non-cash. Total loans and borrowings fell to S$4.45 billion from S$5.48 billion as Singapore development loans were repaid, the debt-to-assets ratio improved to 0.39 times from 0.44, and net asset value per share rose to S$3.95 from S$3.90. On underlying performance the dividend is comfortably covered.

Both things are true at once: the distribution is well supported by cash flow and balance sheet, and it is still more than the company reported earning.

Tower REIT, seven days before the year end

Investment properties fell S$105.9 million over the year despite S$82.2 million of fair value gains. The roll-forward reconciles exactly: S$6,970.8 million opening, plus S$19.0 million of additions, less S$255.1 million on a deemed disposal, plus S$82.2 million of fair value change, plus S$48.0 million of translation, giving S$6,864.9 million.

The deemed disposal is Tower REIT. On 23rd June 2026, seven days before the year end, GuocoLand’s 68 per cent-owned subsidiary GuocoLand (Malaysia) Berhad sold a 20 per cent equity interest in GLM REIT Management, the manager of Tower REIT, to a wholly owned subsidiary of Hong Leong Company (Malaysia) Berhad. GuocoLand (Malaysia) retained 80 per cent. The group reassessed control, concluded that decisions over relevant activities now require joint approval, and reclassified Tower REIT from subsidiary to joint venture — derecognising its assets, liabilities and non-controlling interests and recognising the retained interest at fair value. The transaction is disclosed in note 11 of the financial statements. It does not appear in the results release.

Singapore accounts for 79 per cent of total assets. Fair value gains on investment properties were S$82.2 million against S$58.9 million, of which S$81.1 million arose in the investment segment. Management fee income rose more than fivefold to S$12.7 million from S$2.4 million. The group also recorded a gain on the disposal of the Thistle Johor Bahru hotel in Malaysia. Lentor Modern mall opened in January 2026 and its leases commence progressively.

Three things this article does not tell you

Year-on-year movement per building is not computable from public disclosure. The annual report names the valuers and the dates but gives no per-asset values; only a prior-year valuation notice would supply the 30th June 2025 base, and PropertyAtlas does not hold one.

Nor are per-square-foot values available on a like-for-like basis. The gross floor area disclosed for Guoco Tower, 157,738 square metres, includes Wallich Residence and the Sofitel Singapore City Centre, while the valuation notice covers office and retail alone. Dividing one by the other would produce a number that means nothing.

And Guoco Changfeng City is stated at RMB 2,934.0 million because that is the currency in which it was valued. No Singapore dollar equivalent is disclosed, and imputing one at a rate of our choosing would put a figure into circulation that the company never published.
