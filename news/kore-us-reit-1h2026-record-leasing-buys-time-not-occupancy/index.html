Record Leasing Cuts KORE's 2026 Expiry Wall by More Than Half — But Occupancy Moved 0.2 Points and Bridge Crossing's Anchor Is Handing Back 116,000 sf

KORE US REIT signed more space in the first half of 2026 than in any first half since listing, and almost all of it went into re-papering near-term expiries rather than filling empty floors. Committed occupancy moved two-tenths of a point. The distribution…

By PropertyAtlas·28 July 2026·7 min read
Photo: Westech 360, Austin, Texas — one of KORE US REIT's 13 freehold US office assets. Credit: KORE US REIT.

KORE US REIT reported results for the six months to 30 June 2026 on 28 July 2026, and the leasing number is the one the manager led with. The trust signed 550,522 sf in the first half — 11.5% of net lettable area, and its strongest first half since listing in November 2017 — with 492,365 sf of that in the second quarter alone. Adjusted net property income rose 10.2% to US$45.6 million, net property income 6.1% to US$43.1 million, and income available for distribution 2.1% to US$20.4 million. Committed portfolio occupancy moved two-tenths of a point, to 85.3%.

Those two facts sit oddly together, and the reconciliation is the story. Record leasing that barely moves occupancy is leasing that holds ground rather than gains it.

What the record leasing actually bought

Renewals made up 90.6% of second-quarter volume. The payoff shows up in the expiry profile rather than in the occupancy line. Expiries falling due in 2026 dropped from 9.5% of net lettable area at the end of March to 4.2%; 2027 fell from 20.2% to 16.8%.

On a 4.803 million sf portfolio those two reductions are worth roughly 255,000 sf and 163,000 sf respectively — about 418,000 sf in total, which accounts for the overwhelming majority of the 492,365 sf signed during the quarter. Read correctly, this was a defensive half executed unusually well. Rental reversion held at a positive 1.5% for both the quarter and the half, the portfolio carries built-in annual escalations of 2.6%, and weighted average lease expiry is 3.9 years by committed rental income.

The Westpark Portfolio is the persistent drag. Occupancy there has fallen from 92.7% in the third quarter of 2025 to 73.0%, roughly 154,000 sf given up on a 784,000 sf asset. What makes that specific rather than cyclical is the submarket around it: CoStar has Redmond running at 5.7% vacancy against Westpark’s own 27.0%, with Microsoft building three million sf in the same submarket. This is an asset problem, not a market problem.

Austin carried the quarter in the other direction. Great Hills Plaza added 5.2 points and Westech 360 1.5 points, both against an Austin Northwest submarket sitting at 17.2% vacancy.

The give-back that is not in the occupancy line

Bridge Crossing in Nashville shows 100.0% committed occupancy, and has done for four consecutive quarters. The results presentation also discloses that the manager negotiated an early lease renewal with the building’s main tenant, preserving 69,000 sf of occupancy despite a reduction in footprint of 116,000 sf.

Both statements are accurate. The reduction has not yet taken effect and so does not appear in the 30 June figure. Bridge Crossing has 199,000 sf of net lettable area, which makes 116,000 sf equal to 58% of the building. Once the reduction lands, the asset moves from 100% occupied to roughly 42% — worth about 2.4 points of portfolio occupancy on its own.

The tenant is Comdata, listed in the top-ten table at 2.5% of committed rental income and 3.9% of net lettable area, or approximately 187,000 sf. That is effectively the entire building. Bridge Crossing carries US$44.0 million of fair value and Nashville contributes 5.4% of net property income. No timing for the reduction is disclosed anywhere in the release.

The 42% figure is PropertyAtlas arithmetic on disclosed inputs. The manager has published no pro-forma occupancy for Bridge Crossing.

For context, the manager’s stated target is to finish FY2026 with occupancy broadly in line with the 87.2% recorded at end-2025. That requires roughly 91,000 sf of net absorption in the second half, before any Bridge Crossing effect.

A 21% payout that is funding capital expenditure

Income available for distribution was US$20.366 million across 1,044,450,254 units, or 1.95 US cents per unit for the half. The manager declared 0.40 cents, a payout ratio of about 21%, and retained US$16.2 million.

That retention has a destination. KORE paid US$20.6 million of capital expenditure on its investment properties during the half, against the US$16.2 million retained. The 21% payout is funding the asset enhancement and spec-suite programme: the completed lobby repositioning at 10800 The Plaza Buildings, The Post spec floor targeted for third-quarter completion with two suites already leased, and a 43,600 sf spec-suite pipeline due by year-end at roughly 28% pre-let. It is a defensible use of cash. It is also capital allocation rather than distribution policy, and the manager expects the quantum to stay consistent through the second half.

One correction is worth making, because the presentation invites the error. The 22.6% fall in unit price is measured from 31 December 2025. Measured from 31 March 2026, the units are up, from US$0.177 to US$0.182. The de-rating happened in the first quarter, not the second.

Leverage rests on last December’s mark

Net asset value per unit rose to US$0.70 from US$0.68. That is retained earnings, not asset appreciation. Investment properties increased 1.6% to US$1,346.5 million, and the entire movement is US$21.1 million of capitalised capital expenditure. The fair-value change for the half was nil.

The carrying value is the 31 December 2025 independent valuation, adjusted for capital expenditure, tenant improvements, leasing costs and capitalised straight-line rent. A full valuation follows for the year ending 31 December 2026, and the notes carry explicit language on material valuation uncertainty. FY2025 produced a US$43.5 million fair-value loss.

That matters because aggregate leverage of 43.3% is gross borrowings over total assets, and total assets carry the December mark.

The near-term maturity picture needs care. The media release states there are no term-loan maturities remaining in 2026 and no term-loan refinancing requirements until 2027. The balance sheet states that US$187.0 million of borrowings is repayable within twelve months of 30 June 2026 — US$122.5 million of term loans, US$39.5 million drawn on an uncommitted revolving facility and US$25.0 million on a committed one. Both are true: the twelve-month window runs into 2027 while the manager’s framing is calendar-year. Note 2.2 discloses that Group current liabilities exceed current assets by US$171.9 million and sets out the going-concern reasoning. Against that sit US$82.3 million of committed undrawn facilities, US$55.5 million uncommitted, and the new US$40.0 million committed revolver secured in the second quarter.

The rest of the capital structure is conservative. Weighted average term to maturity is 1.7 years and interest coverage 2.5 times, falling to 2.1 times on a 100 basis point rate rise and 2.2 times on a 10% decline in EBITDA. All-in cost of debt is 4.91%, or 4.74% excluding upfront costs. All borrowings are unsecured and US dollar-denominated, and 58.2% is hedged floating-to-fixed excluding the uncommitted facility. Every 50 basis point move in SOFR is worth about US$1.38 million a year to distributable income.

Quality where it counts, exposure where it does not

Eight of the twelve submarket comparisons in the presentation show KORE’s asset tighter than the market around it, several by a wide margin: One Twenty Five at 3.0% vacant against Las Colinas at 21.2%, Maitland Promenade at 4.3% against 15.9%, 1800 West Loop South at 18.2% against a Houston Galleria/Uptown submarket at 35.1%, and Great Hills Plaza at 7.1% against 17.2%. That is a real quality signal, and it supports the flight-to-quality framing the manager leans on.

Four run looser than their submarket, and that is where the exposure sits: The Westpark Portfolio at 27.0% against 5.7%, Bellaire Park at 19.5% against 16.3%, Westmoor Center at 19.4% against 12.8%, and Iron Point at 17.4% against 8.8%. Bellevue Technology Center is line-ball at 6.5% against 5.8%. Westpark is the outlier by an order of magnitude, and the manager has said it is assessing the portfolio for optimisation, including selective divestment of assets that do not meet long-term growth priorities, with proceeds applied to debt reduction or redeployment.

What to watch

Three things. Whether the Bridge Crossing footprint reduction is dated in the third-quarter update, because it is the largest identified swing in the portfolio and it is currently invisible in the headline occupancy figure. Whether Westpark stabilises, given a Redmond submarket at 5.7% vacancy that offers the asset no excuse. And the December valuation, which will reset the leverage ratio for the first time in a year.

The distribution has a record date of 5 August 2026 and is payable on 29 September 2026. Unitholders who do not submit valid US tax forms by 31 August 2026 are subject to 30% withholding.
