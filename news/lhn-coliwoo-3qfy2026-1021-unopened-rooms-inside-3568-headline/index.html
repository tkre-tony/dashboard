LHN Limited And Coliwoo Holdings Both Headline 3,568 Co-Living Rooms And Both Footnote 1,021 Under Renovation — The Four Projects In LHN's Pipeline Table Sum To Exactly 1,021, So 28.6 Per Cent Of The Advertised Portfolio Is Not Open, And Nothing Was Added In The Quarter

LHN Limited and Coliwoo Holdings Limited both report a 3,568-room co-living portfolio at 30th June 2026, and both footnote that 1,021 of those rooms are under renovation. The pipeline table in LHN's own presentation lists four projects — 380, 368, 153 and…

By PropertyAtlas·27 August 2026·8 min read
Photo: Coliwoo Resort Changi at 159 Jalan Loyang Besar, the group's first resort-style co-living property, which commenced operations in July 2026 and added 380 rooms to the number actually open. The headline portfolio did not change, because those rooms were already counted inside the 3,568 reported for the quarter ended 30th June 2026. Credit: Coliwoo Holdings Limited.

LHN Limited and Coliwoo Holdings Limited both told the market this week that the Coliwoo portfolio stands at 3,568 rooms. Both footnoted the same qualification: 1,021 of those rooms are under renovation. The pipeline table in LHN’s own presentation lists four projects — 159 Jalan Loyang Besar at 380 rooms, 2 Changi Business Park Avenue 1 at 368, 1 King George’s Avenue at 153 and 50 Armenian Street at 120. They sum to 1,021. Every unopened room the group holds is already counted inside the headline number.

Coliwoo filed its voluntary business update on 25th August; LHN filed its own the following evening. Neither states an operational room count. It is arithmetic on two figures each of them discloses: 3,568 less 1,021 leaves 2,547 rooms actually open, and 28.6 per cent of the advertised portfolio is not.

The proportion has widened rather than narrowed. At 30th September 2025 the group carried 714 rooms under renovation within a 2,933-room portfolio, or 24.3 per cent. Headline rooms have since risen 21.7 per cent; rooms actually open have risen 14.8 per cent.

Coliwoo’s half-year presentation in May carried a further line against the same 1,021: the majority, it said, was expected to be ready and operational by FY2026. The pipeline table three slides later in that same deck dates 380 rooms to FY2026 and the remaining 641 to FY2027 and FY2028. Thirty-seven per cent is not a majority, and the table sat in the same document as the claim. The 380 has since moved too. Both presentations date 159 Jalan Loyang Besar to the third quarter; LHN’s August release states that the property launched in July 2026, which falls in the fourth.

The operational base is the one that reproduces the issuers’ own averages

Coliwoo discloses occupancy by segment and an average across the portfolio. Weighting the segment rates by the rooms in each segment ought to return the average. On the headline 3,568-room base it does not. On the 2,547-room operational base it does — in both of the last two reported periods.

The residual on the operational base is 0.27 and 0.51 percentage points, consistent with an average struck across a quarter against a room split reported at a single date. On the headline base it is 0.80 and 2.54 points. Two periods, the same direction. The figures in the two right-hand columns are PropertyAtlas calculations; only the final column is disclosed.

What that base implies for Coliwoo Midtown

Coliwoo reports 93.7 per cent including Coliwoo Midtown and 96.0 per cent excluding it. The property has 212 rooms. Backing the one figure out of the other across the operational base gives an implied average of approximately 68 per cent for Midtown over the quarter. It is a derivation, not a disclosure, and it is sensitive to the base: run on the headline 3,568 the same arithmetic returns about 57 per cent. The operational base is the one that reproduces the issuer’s stated averages twice, and it cross-checks — it implies the other 283 owned rooms ran at roughly 90 per cent, against a disclosed owned-segment average of 80.8 per cent.

Both companies disclose that Midtown reached close to 90 per cent in July 2026. July falls after the quarter closed. PropertyAtlas reported the sale and leaseback of Midtown to CapitaLand Ascott Trust for S$134.0 million on 8th August; the tenure, the Rule 1006 tests and the leaseback economics are set out there and are not repeated here.

Nothing was added in the quarter

The portfolio at 30th June is identical to the portfolio at 31st March: 28 properties split 12 owned, 11 leased and five managed; 3,568 rooms split 1,136, 1,907 and 525; 1,021 under renovation. Coliwoo states the property count is unchanged. No rooms were added during the third quarter.

That makes the stated target arithmetic rather than ambition. Both companies restate an intention to reach approximately 4,000 rooms in Singapore by the end of 2026 — about 432 above the current headline. Every remaining pipeline room is already inside the 3,568 and every one of them is dated to the first quarter of FY2027 or later. Coliwoo Resort Changi opening in July lifts rooms actually open to 2,927 but leaves the headline where it was, because those 380 were already counted. Roughly 432 rooms would have to come from transactions neither company has announced. Neither release specifies whether “the end of 2026” means the calendar year or the financial year, which closes on 30th September.

The leased segment softened, and it has no ramp-up to explain it

Owned occupancy fell 13.0 points, from 93.8 per cent to 80.8 per cent, which is Midtown. Managed held at 99.6 per cent. Leased fell from 96.7 per cent to 95.0 per cent — 1.7 points across roughly 1,527 open rooms with no new opening inside them. Coliwoo’s release describes sustained occupancy across its leased and managed segments. Managed was flat. Leased was not.

A carpark valued at S$28.5 million returns S$4.5 million

LHN’s presentation carries a capital-recycling slide with two transactions. The second reads: property, Golden Mile Carpark; transaction value, S$28.5 million. The filed press release is more precise. What LHN is divesting is its entire 50 per cent interest in Metropolitan Parking Pte. Ltd., which owns the 540-lot carpark at 6001 Beach Road, Golden Mile Tower. The transaction values the carpark property at S$28.5 million, and LHN expects to receive approximately S$4.5 million of net proceeds. The announcement was made on 7th August 2026.

Half of S$28.5 million is S$14.25 million. LHN receives S$4.5 million, or 15.8 per cent of the headline figure. The difference is presumably debt and costs at the joint-venture level, though neither document breaks it out. A reader working from the slide alone would overstate what reaches the parent by roughly six times. The buyer is not named, the joint venture’s book value is not given, and no gain or loss is disclosed; the transaction announcement itself has not been read for this article.

The carpark portfolio moves the same way. LHN reports 107 carparks in operation at 30th June, having added seven during the quarter. Its half-year presentation put the count at 103 at 31st March. Seven added against a net gain of four means three carparks left the portfolio, which neither document mentions. On lots the arithmetic points the same way — roughly 900 out against more than 1,500 added — but both lot figures are stated as approximate, where the carpark counts are not.

The 13.7 per cent revenue fall is one line

LHN’s group revenue for the half to 31st March was S$60.9 million against S$70.6 million, a fall of 13.7 per cent. The Property Development segment went from S$12.110 million to nil. That single line is S$12.110 million against a group decline of S$9.640 million. Every other segment together added S$2.470 million, or 4.2 per cent. Space Optimisation, the core, rose 10.7 per cent. Profit attributable to shareholders rose 18.6 per cent to S$16.8 million, earnings per share to 3.92 cents, gross margin 3.3 points to 60.9 per cent, and gearing fell to 53.2 per cent. A reader taking the headline decline at face value would have the business backwards.

Two companies, one business, opposite signs

LHN’s residential segment — which is Coliwoo plus the 329-room 85 SOHO portfolio — reported revenue up 16.0 per cent and adjusted profit before taxation down 32.4 per cent, from S$16.894 million to S$11.414 million. Coliwoo, reporting the same business over the same six months, headlined adjusted PATMI up 13.9 per cent.

The definitions differ. LHN adjusts for fair value movements and one-off items. Coliwoo adjusts for those and also reallocates a S$7.432 million gain on net investment in subleases that was recognised upfront in the prior-year half, spreading it across a three-year sublease term. Remove that gain from LHN’s comparative base and the residential segment moves from minus 32.4 per cent to approximately plus 20.6 per cent — the same direction as the subsidiary. This is a reconciliation, not a correction of either issuer: LHN’s residential segment also carries 85 SOHO, and its segment revenue of S$27.440 million sits S$570,000 above Coliwoo’s standalone S$26.870 million without explanation in either document.

The stake is two-thirds of the parent

LHN put its own market capitalisation at approximately S$279 million on 21st May and approximately S$244 million on 25th August, a fall of 12.5 per cent across the summer. Coliwoo moved the other way: approximately S$240 million on 15th May, and S$248.8 million on 6th August, the figure used for the Rule 1006 market-capitalisation test in the Midtown announcement.

LHN holds 65 per cent of Coliwoo. On the 6th August figure that stake is worth approximately S$161.7 million — 66.3 per cent of LHN’s entire market capitalisation nineteen days later. What the market implies for everything else the group owns, on those two dates, is roughly S$82 million: fourteen industrial properties, five commercial, the 2,186 storage units at Work+Store, 134 facilities-management clients, 107 carparks, 12.3 megawatts of solar capacity and the development book. Both figures are issuer-stated approximations, taken nineteen days apart, and neither is a valuation.

There is one more thing about description worth recording. LHN calls Midtown a 212-room serviced apartment property. Coliwoo calls it a 212-room co-living development. Coliwoo’s own half-year presentation calls it a co-living serviced apartment. Coliwoo names CapitaLand Ascott Trust as the counterparty; LHN’s release does not name the buyer at all. Both companies disclosed what was required of them, twenty-seven hours apart. The emphasis differs.
