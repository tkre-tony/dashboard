Digital Core REIT Will Sell US$315.9 Million Of Wholly-Controlled North American Assets To Its Sponsor And Buy Minority Stakes In Singapore And Osaka — The 4.1 Per Cent DPU Accretion Is Measured Against A Lease That Has Not Commenced, And 1.2 Points Of It Comes From A Unit Buyback

Digital Core REIT will sell its entire interests in Toronto and Los Angeles and 39 per cent of Northern Virginia to its own sponsor, and redeploy into a 2.5 per cent slice of a Singapore data centre and an incremental 25 per cent of an Osaka facility.…

By PropertyAtlas·12 August 2026·10 min read
Photo: Of the 15.6 points of total uplift Digital Core REIT displays against its FY2025 distribution, 11.1 arrive from the 8217 Linton Hall Road lease commencing 1st December 2026 — whether or not this transaction proceeds. Illustration: PropertyAtlas. Source: Digital Core REIT announcement and investor presentation, 12th August 2026.

Digital Core REIT will sell US$315.9 million of North American data centres to its own sponsor and buy US$176.1 million of minority interests in Singapore and Osaka from the same sponsor. Every leg of the transaction is with Digital Realty. There is no third party anywhere in it. The announcement, media release and investor presentation were released on 12th August 2026.

The transaction, plainly

Digital Core REIT will sell its 90 per cent interest in 371 Gough Road in Toronto for US$126.9 million, its 90 per cent interest in 200 North Nash Street in Los Angeles for US$78.6 million, and a 39 per cent interest in 8217 Linton Hall Road in Northern Virginia for US$110.4 million. Aggregate consideration is US$315.9 million. The buyer in all three cases is Digital CR Singapore Investor, LLC, a wholly owned subsidiary of Digital Realty.

It will acquire a 2.5 per cent interest in Digital Loyang 2 at 11 Loyang Close, Singapore, for S$87.4 million, and an incremental 25 per cent of Digital Osaka 3 for ¥17.6 billion, taking that holding from 20 to 45 per cent. Aggregate consideration is US$176.1 million. The seller in both cases is a wholly owned subsidiary of Digital Realty.

The transaction is 77.2 per cent of market capitalisation under Rule 1006(c) and 35.6 per cent of net profits under Rule 1006(b), making it a major transaction under Rule 1014(1). It is simultaneously an interested person transaction under Chapter 9 and an interested party transaction under the Property Funds Appendix. Unitholder approval is required and Deloitte has been appointed as independent financial adviser.

The assets go out at book

The three disposals generate an estimated net gain after tax of approximately US$3.4 million — US$0.6 million on Los Angeles, nil on Toronto, and US$2.8 million on Northern Virginia. That is 1.1 per cent of the consideration. There is no capital gain story in this transaction.

Toronto sells at exactly the appraisal. Cushman & Wakefield and BBG both valued 371 Gough Road at C$200.0 million at 100 per cent share, and the 90 per cent interest goes for C$180.0 million. In local currency the asset has not moved: the FY2025 results presentation carried it at C$200.0 million at 31st December 2025 as well. What changed is the exchange rate — the US dollar figure fell because the Canadian dollar rate moved from 1.370 to 1.419.

Los Angeles is the anomaly. The FY2025 presentation carried 200 North Nash Street at US$65.0 million at 100 per cent share as at 31st December 2025. Cushman & Wakefield now values it at US$86.6 million, an increase of 33 per cent in six months. Over the same window occupancy fell from 91.6 per cent at 30th September 2025 to 83.5 per cent at 31st December and 81.7 per cent at 30th June 2026, and the weighted average lease expiry shortened from 1.1 years to 0.8 years. The disposal nonetheless produces an estimated gain of US$0.6 million against a 90 per cent share carrying value of US$58.5 million at last December. The announcement does not reconcile these figures, and this publication does not speculate as to why.

Where the accretion actually comes from

The headline is 4.1 per cent distribution per unit accretion. The base is not the 3.60 US cents Digital Core REIT actually distributed for FY2025. The base is 4.00 cents — a pro forma figure that assumes the ten-year lease at 8217 Linton Hall Road, which does not commence until 1st December 2026, had been running for the whole of 2025.

Of the 15.6 per cent total uplift the presentation displays off 3.60 cents, 11.1 points is the Linton Hall lease-up — which happens whether or not this transaction proceeds — 1.7 points is the asset swap, and 1.2 points is repurchasing units below net asset value.

The leverage figure is drawn from the same column and is therefore internally consistent, but it runs the other way. On the transaction alone, aggregate leverage falls from 39.2 per cent to 35.2 per cent, a 400 basis point improvement. The buyback puts 110 basis points back on, producing the 36.3 per cent and the 290 basis points in the headline. The same US$20 million of repurchases is flattering the distribution figure and diluting the leverage figure at once.

Net asset value per unit falls on the transaction, from US$0.79 to US$0.78. The buyback is what restores it to US$0.79.

The debt trade inside the property trade

The media release describes repaying approximately US$117 million of debt. The investor presentation’s uses chart shows US dollar and euro denominated debt repayment at 59 per cent of US$496 million, or approximately US$293 million. Both are correct. The first is net, the second gross.

Reconciled: Digital Core REIT will repay roughly US$293 million of US dollar and euro borrowings while raising US$176.1 million of new yen and Singapore dollar borrowings, for a net reduction of US$117.4 million.

At 31st December 2025 the trust carried US$671 million of gross borrowings, 26 per cent in US dollars, 52 per cent in euro and 22 per cent in yen. This transaction therefore retires something close to half of the US dollar and euro stack and re-denominates it into yen and Singapore dollars. Given the spread between yen and US dollar funding costs, a portion of the disclosed accretion is a financing outcome rather than a property outcome. No document in the pack characterises it that way. Weighted average debt maturity also shortens, from 3.7 years at FY2025 to 3.3 years pro forma.

The sponsor’s discount has gone

In December 2024, Digital Realty sold Digital Core REIT an additional 15.1 per cent of the Frankfurt facility at an agreed value of €470 million — a 19 per cent discount to the JLL valuation and 16.5 per cent to Newmark. That transaction lifted the holding from 49.9 to 65 per cent and delivered 3.1 per cent distribution accretion and 5.0 per cent net asset value accretion.

In March 2025 the sponsor sold a 20 per cent interest in Digital Osaka 3 for ¥13.0 billion, implying ¥65.0 billion at 100 per cent share. Today the incremental 25 per cent of the same asset is priced at ¥17.6 billion, implying ¥70.5 billion — 8.5 per cent higher in yen across seventeen months, and 1.0 per cent below the fresh Cushman & Wakefield valuation of ¥71.24 billion. Digital Loyang 2 is agreed at Cushman & Wakefield’s valuation to the dollar.

Two observations follow. The first is that in US dollar terms the Osaka repricing vanishes: the March 2025 tranche cost US$4.335 million per percentage point of interest, and this one costs US$4.34 million. Yen appreciation absorbed the entire increase. The second is that a sponsor which sold Frankfurt at roughly a fifth below appraised value now sells at par. Whatever else this transaction is, it is not the Frankfurt trade repeated.

A 2.5 per cent entry, and the end of the freehold portfolio

“Establishing presence in Singapore” describes a 2.5 per cent interest in a single asset. On a look-through basis that is roughly 8,600 square feet of the 344,445 square foot facility and about 1,053 kilowatts of its 42,100 kilowatt IT load. Singapore becomes 4 per cent of assets under management.

Digital Core REIT is also not the first Singapore-listed trust to hold a Singapore data centre. Keppel DC REIT has held them for years. This is Digital Core REIT’s maiden Singapore exposure, not a market first.

It carries a structural consequence the pack does not mention. Digital Loyang 2 sits on a thirty-year leasehold interest commencing 9th March 2019, with 23 years remaining. The FY2025 results presentation led its portfolio page with 100 per cent freehold assets and used freehold share as the competitive differentiator against peers — Digital Core REIT at 100 per cent, against 83 per cent for NTT DC REIT, 89 per cent for Mapletree Industrial Trust, 44 per cent for Keppel DC REIT and 17 per cent for CapitaLand Ascendas REIT. That claim ends with this transaction.

The governance terms of the Singapore stake

The joint venture agreement gives the property company a board of no more than three directors, all appointed by the vendor. The trustee may request board minutes and appoint observers, who do not count towards quorum and cannot vote. In exchange, at least 90 per cent of net profits legally available for distribution must be paid out in proportion to shareholding, and a list of reserved matters requires unanimous shareholder approval.

That is a defensible structure for a 2.5 per cent holder. It is also closer to a preferred income instrument than to a property investment, and it is the clearest expression of a direction this publication has been tracking since April.

The direction of travel

When PropertyAtlas covered the 1H 2026 results on 29th July, the finding was that a flat 1.80 US cent distribution was not being produced by the property portfolio. Net property income had fallen 5.7 per cent and cash net property income 7.1 per cent. Cash distributions from two 20 per cent held Osaka joint ventures had nearly tripled to US$10.5 million and supplied 30.1 per cent of distributable income. A buyback closed the remaining gap.

This transaction converts that pattern into strategy. Digital Core REIT sells the assets over which it has control — 90 per cent of Toronto, 90 per cent of Los Angeles, 39 per cent of Northern Virginia — and acquires minority positions it does not control, at 45 per cent and 2.5 per cent. It funds a further US$20 million of buybacks. The two engines that carried the last distribution are the two engines this transaction enlarges.

A trust that listed in 2021 holding 90 per cent stakes in ten freehold North American data centres is becoming something structurally different: a holder of minority interests in Asian joint ventures, drawing an increasing share of its distribution from cash distributions rather than consolidated net property income. Whether that is prudent recycling or a change in the nature of the instrument is the question the circular should answer.

Reducing North American exposure from 65 to 52 per cent of assets under management, doubling Asia Pacific from 11 to 22 per cent, and preserving upside by retaining 51 per cent of the Northern Virginia asset whose signed ten-year lease begins in December are all genuine. So is the fact that the sponsor is taking 49 per cent of that de-risked lease-up at a 2.6 per cent premium to valuation.

The manager’s economics

The manager earns 1.0 per cent on the acquisitions and 0.5 per cent on the divestments, approximately US$1.8 million and US$1.6 million respectively, or US$3.4 million in total. Because every leg is an interested party transaction, both must be settled in units issued at prevailing market price and locked up for one year.

US$3.4 million is 7.3 per cent of FY2025 distributable income of US$46.8 million, earned on a single transaction. The trust deed permits it, both fees are disclosed, and the structure is standard. It is nonetheless worth stating that the manager is paid to buy from the sponsor and paid again to sell to the sponsor within the same set of agreements.

One question the circular will need to answer

The FY2025 results presentation records that the sponsor provided a five-year interest-free loan to backstop a near-term cash flow shortfall arising from a customer bankruptcy in Toronto, and helped resolve what was then Digital Core REIT’s second largest customer bankruptcy. The 31st December 2025 balance sheet carries a shareholder loan of US$123.3 million.

371 Gough Road now returns to that sponsor. The announcement’s conditions precedent cover termination of the property management agreement and the Canadian asset management agreement. They do not address the shareholder loan.

The read

This is a well-constructed transaction that does most of what it says: it reduces North American concentration, brings forward Asia Pacific weighting, retains the Northern Virginia upside, and lowers leverage. What the pack does not say is that a majority of the headline accretion was already coming regardless, that a unit buyback is doing double duty on two separate metrics, and that roughly half the US dollar and euro debt stack is being re-denominated into cheaper currencies inside a transaction presented as portfolio recycling. Unitholders voting at the extraordinary general meeting are being asked to approve a change in what the trust owns and, less visibly, a change in how it is funded.
