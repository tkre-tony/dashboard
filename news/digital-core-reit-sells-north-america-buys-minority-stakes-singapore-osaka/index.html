Digital Core REIT Will Sell US$316 Million Of Wholly-Controlled North American Assets To Its Sponsor And Buy Minority Stakes In Singapore And Osaka — 11.1 Of The 15.6 Points Of Headline Uplift Come From A Lease That Has Not Commenced, And Digital Osaka 3 Is Agreed 3.6 Per Cent Below Its Own Carrying Value

Digital Core REIT will sell its entire interests in Toronto and Los Angeles and 39 per cent of Northern Virginia to its own sponsor, and redeploy into a 2.5 per cent slice of a Singapore data centre and an incremental 25 per cent of an Osaka facility.…

By PropertyAtlas·12 August 2026·7 min read
Photo: Of the 15.6 points of total uplift Digital Core REIT displays against its FY2025 distribution, 11.1 arrive from the 8217 Linton Hall Road lease commencing 1st December 2026 — whether or not this transaction proceeds. Illustration: PropertyAtlas. Source: Digital Core REIT announcement and investor presentation, 12th August 2026.

Digital Core REIT will sell interests in three North American data centres to its sponsor for approximately US$316 million and buy minority interests in Singapore and Osaka from the same sponsor for approximately US$176 million. Every leg of the transaction is with Digital Realty. There is no third party anywhere in it.

The transaction, plainly

Digital Core REIT will sell its 90 per cent interest in 371 Gough Road in Toronto for C$180 million, or US$127 million; its 90 per cent interest in 200 North Nash Street in Los Angeles for US$79 million; and a 39 per cent interest in 8217 Linton Hall Road in Northern Virginia for US$110 million. Gross proceeds are approximately US$316 million.

It will acquire a 2.5 per cent interest in 11 Loyang Close in Singapore for S$87 million, or US$68 million, and an incremental 25 per cent of Digital Osaka 3, taking that holding from 20 to 45 per cent, for ¥17.6 billion, or US$108 million. Total investment is approximately US$176 million.

Net proceeds of approximately US$140 million are earmarked as to roughly US$117 million for debt repayment and up to US$20 million for open-market unit repurchases. The transaction requires unitholder approval and is expected to close before year-end.

Everything trades at carrying value — except Osaka

The four assets carry agreed values disclosed at 100 per cent share. Set against the carrying values in the 1H 2026 portfolio table, three of the four are effectively flat.

The asset Digital Core REIT is buying more of is the one priced furthest from its own books, and it is priced below them. Whether that reflects the minority nature of the interest, movement in the yen, or the negotiation itself, the announcement does not say.

One word in the footnotes carries weight here. These are not appraisals. The presentation states that each agreed value was negotiated on a willing-buyer and willing-seller basis, taking into account historical cash flows, in-place rental rates and the underlying customer base alongside current market conditions. In a transaction where the sponsor sits on both sides of all five legs, the distinction between a negotiated value and an independent valuation is not a technicality. The 1H 2026 financial statements record that the portfolio’s independent valuations date from 31st December 2025 and that the Manager considers them still appropriate at 30th June 2026.

Where the accretion comes from

The headline is 4.1 per cent distribution per unit accretion. The presentation’s own bridge shows what sits underneath it: FY2025 as reported at 3.60 US cents, a Linton Hall lease-up step of 0.40 cents, and a pro forma figure of 4.16 cents. The total displayed uplift is 15.6 per cent.

Of the 15.6 points, 11.1 are the Linton Hall lease-up. That lease was announced on 5th January 2026 and commences on 1st December 2026. It is expected to generate approximately US$14.8 million of annualised net property income, around US$13.3 million at Digital Core REIT’s 90 per cent share, and roughly a 35 per cent increase on the previous net rent. It arrives whether or not this transaction proceeds.

The 4.1 per cent is therefore measured against 4.00 cents, not against the 3.60 cents actually distributed for FY2025. The presentation does not disclose how much of the remainder comes from the asset swap and how much from the US$20 million of unit repurchases, both of which sit inside the same pro forma. Aggregate leverage is shown improving 290 basis points, from 39.2 per cent at 30th June 2026 to 36.3 per cent, on that same combined basis.

The debt trade inside the property trade

The sources-and-uses chart is drawn on a US$496 million base. On the uses side, 59 per cent — approximately US$293 million — is repayment of US dollar and euro denominated debt. On the sources side, new yen debt is 22 per cent and new Singapore dollar debt 14 per cent, together roughly US$178 million, raised to fund the Osaka and Singapore acquisitions respectively. Netted, that is the approximately US$117 million of debt paydown the media release describes.

The scale is easier to see against the balance sheet. At 30th June 2026 gross borrowings stood at US$715.9 million, of which US dollar and euro facilities accounted for US$572.5 million. Repaying US$293 million retires 51.2 per cent of that stack and re-denominates the funding into yen and Singapore dollars. Given the spread between yen and US dollar funding costs, a portion of the disclosed accretion is a financing outcome rather than a property outcome. No document in the pack characterises it that way.

The sources side also shows units issued for transaction fees at 1 per cent of the total, approximately US$5 million.

A 2.5 per cent entry, and the end of the freehold portfolio

“Establishing presence in Singapore” describes a 2.5 per cent interest in a single asset. On a look-through basis that is roughly 8,611 square feet of the 344,445 square foot facility and about 1,053 kilowatts of its 42,100 kilowatt IT load. Singapore becomes 4 per cent of assets under management.

It carries a structural consequence the pack does not mention. 11 Loyang Close is held on a leasehold interest with 23 years remaining. The 1H 2026 results presentation led its portfolio page with 100 per cent freehold assets, and the April 2026 business update used freehold share as the competitive differentiator against peers — Digital Core REIT at 100 per cent, against 89 per cent for Mapletree Industrial Trust, 83 per cent for NTT DC REIT, 44 per cent for Keppel DC REIT and 15 per cent for CapitaLand Ascendas REIT. That claim ends with this transaction.

The direction of travel

When PropertyAtlas covered the 1H 2026 results on 29th July, the finding was that a flat 1.80 US cent distribution was not being produced by the property portfolio. Net property income had fallen 5.7 per cent and cash net property income 7.1 per cent. Distributions received from the two 20 per cent held Osaka associates had risen from US$3.8 million to US$10.5 million year on year, against a share of associates’ results of only US$3.5 million.

This transaction extends that pattern. Digital Core REIT sells the assets over which it has control — 90 per cent of Toronto, 90 per cent of Los Angeles, 39 per cent of Northern Virginia — and acquires positions it does not control, at 45 per cent and 2.5 per cent. It funds a further US$20 million of buybacks. The two engines that carried the last distribution are the two engines this transaction enlarges.

What the transaction genuinely delivers is real. North American exposure falls from 65 to 52 per cent of assets under management and Asia Pacific doubles from 11 to 22 per cent. Investment-grade rent contribution rises from 83 to 86 per cent. Retaining 51 per cent of Linton Hall preserves the upside of a signed ten-year lease that begins in December. Leverage improves.

The shareholder loan

Digital Core REIT’s balance sheet carries a shareholder loan of US$119.9 million at 30th June 2026, down from US$123.3 million at 31st December 2025. It is not interest-free: the 1H 2026 accounts record US$2.0 million of interest expense on it, and US$2.0 million of interest expense to a related party in the same period.

371 Gough Road now returns to that sponsor. The announcement describes the transaction as subject to customary closing conditions including unitholder approval, and does not address the loan.

The read

This is a well-constructed transaction that does most of what it says: it reduces North American concentration, brings forward Asia Pacific weighting, retains the Northern Virginia upside, and lowers leverage. What the pack does not say is that a majority of the headline accretion was already coming regardless, that the split between the asset swap and the buyback inside the remainder is not disclosed, and that roughly half the US dollar and euro debt stack is being re-denominated into cheaper currencies inside a transaction presented as portfolio recycling. Unitholders voting at the extraordinary general meeting are being asked to approve a change in what the trust owns and, less visibly, a change in how it is funded.
