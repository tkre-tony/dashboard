Brokers Have Priced Wee Hur's Tuas View Tenure Risk For A Year. The Planning Record Says It Is Not A Lease But A Temporary Permission — Ceasing 31st October 2026, With The Premises To Revert To Industrial Or Warehouse Use, Worker Numbers Capped On Renewal, And Land Betterment Charge In Prospect.

Wee Hur's first-half profit attributable to equity holders rose 17.1 per cent to S$45.29 million. The tenure risk under Tuas View Dormitory is well covered — one broker applies a four-times multiple to the dormitory business because of it. What has not…

By PropertyAtlas·19 August 2026·11 min read
Photo: Tuas View Dormitory at 70 Tuas South Avenue 1, looking out to the Johor Strait. The asset has operated since 2014 and holds 15,744 of Wee Hur's 26,244 beds at a 60 per cent effective group interest. URA Grant of Written Permission (Temporary) P121213-29J2-E062 provides that the temporary workers' dormitory shall cease with effect from 31st October 2026, and that the premises shall thereafter be used for industrial or warehouse purposes. Credit: Wee Hur Holdings Ltd 1H 2026 Financial Results presentation.

Wee Hur's first-half profit attributable to equity holders rose 17.1 per cent to S$45.29 million. The risk sitting behind that number — the tenure under Tuas View Dormitory, which holds 15,744 of the group's 26,244 beds — is already known and already priced. What has not been published is the mechanism. It is not a land lease in the ordinary sense. It is a temporary planning permission — the seventh decision on a site whose lease began in November 2013 — that ceases on 31st October 2026, and on expiry the premises are to be used for industrial or warehouse purposes.

The market has this as a lease. The permission says something narrower

Brokers covering the stock have modelled the Tuas View expiry for more than a year. One applies a four-times earnings multiple to the workers' dormitory business specifically to reflect the short remaining tenure, and publishes a non-renewal downside case roughly eleven per cent below its target price. Another calls extension highly probable given the asset's scale and Dormitory Transition Scheme compliance. This is not an overlooked risk, and nothing below should be read as suggesting it is.

What the coverage describes as a land lease is, in the planning record, a Grant of Written Permission (Temporary) issued by the Urban Redevelopment Authority under Section 14(4) of the Planning Act. Decision number P121213-29J2-E062, granted 26th January 2024 on an application received 18th January 2024, over MK07 on Lot 04046C, 70 Tuas South Avenue 1, in the Tuas planning area. The named developer is Active System Engineering Pte Ltd — the 60 per cent held operating company for Tuas View — with Goh Yew Tee, Wee Hur's Executive Director and Deputy Managing Director, named on the face of the grant.

Its conditions are more specific than the shorthand suggests.

ConditionWhat it says
Planning condition 3The temporary workers' dormitory shall cease with effect from 31st October 2026. On expiry, "the premises shall be used for industrial or warehouse purposes".
Planning condition 6No increase in the number of workers on any proposed renewal. URA is to be updated on the number staying at the premises at the time of renewal.
Planning condition 2The permission will not be renewed if there is a subsequent redevelopment or additions and alterations proposal increasing the gross floor area of the existing building.
Planning condition 4No land or strata subdivision of the dormitory.
Advisory note 5Renewal must be applied for three months before the expiry date. No reminder letters are sent. An application received after expiry is treated as a fresh application.
Advisory note 7Land Betterment Charge may be leviable on the next renewal application, regardless of whether it was levied on the previous or current approval. Assessed by SLA on rates reviewed by the Chief Valuer every six months.
Advisory note 15Temporary permission is subject to review. Should the use become objectionable, further extension may not be granted.

Three of those have no counterpart in any Wee Hur disclosure. A mandated reversion to industrial or warehouse use is a different proposition from a lease that might not be renewed. A cap on worker numbers at renewal means the larger of the two dormitories cannot grow beds under a renewed permission. And Land Betterment Charge is a cash cost, quantum unknown, that appears in no results document, no annual report and no broker note we have seen.

The permission in evidence is the January 2024 grant. Its authenticity and currency can be checked against the decision number on URA's development register. Whether a renewal application has been lodged is not disclosed anywhere; the group said only, in its results press release, that an update is expected in October.

Thirteen years of lease, six planning decisions, and a one-line disclosure

The record is in the planning file and in Wee Hur's own earlier reporting, and it settles the point.

Decision P121213-29J2-Z000, dated 9th April 2014, on submission reference ES20131212R0187 lodged in December 2013 — same lot, same developer, same agent. It permitted the erection of a temporary workers' dormitory for 16,800 workers, comprising 20 blocks of four-storey dormitory and three blocks of two-storey ancillary administration, recreational and ancillary facilities. Total gross floor area 118,065.12 square metres including 3,500 square metres of commercial space, at a gross plot ratio of 1.40. Against the 83,973 square metres of land recorded in the annual report at page 19, that gross floor area implies a ratio of 1.406 — the two documents agree.

Planning condition (b) of that 2014 grant reads: "The use is approved on a temporary permission for 3 years to cease on 09-04-2017."

Three years, in 2014. The current permission carries the same submission root — 121213-29J2 — and runs to 31st October 2026. Twelve and a half years of temporary permission, granted in successive terms.

URA's development register records six planning decisions on the site: the New Erection of 9th April 2014; renewals of temporary written permission for temporary structures on 13th April 2017, 4th October 2019 and 29th April 2021; a Change of Use on 16th September 2022; and an Extension of Temporary Permission for Change of Use on 17th March 2023. The grant now in force is the seventh.

The lease began in 2013, and the group used to say so

Here is the part that matters most, and it comes from Wee Hur's own annual report — just not the current one.

The FY2020 Properties Portfolio, stated as at 31st March 2021, described Tuas View Dormitory's tenure as a "10-year lease wef 1 November 2013", with gross floor area of 118,065 square metres and a 60 per cent effective group interest. That gross floor area matches the 2014 planning permission to the square metre.

The FY2020 Chairman's message adds the mechanism: "The land lease for Tuas View Dormitory has been extended for another 3 years from 1 November 2020". Ten years from 1st November 2013 runs to 31st October 2023; a three-year extension from 1st November 2020 reaches the same date. The two disclosures reconcile, and imply an initial term of seven years to 31st October 2020.

Then a further three years from 1st November 2023 — which is what the FY2025 annual report reports, and all that it reports.

Tenure as disclosed, by documentWhat it says

The lease commenced on 1st November 2013. It has run thirteen years. The FY2025 annual report gives the tenure in one line and starts the clock in 2023 — no lessor, no commencement, no original term, no renewal history, and no gross floor area, where the FY2020 report carried all but the lessor. Note 19 adds only that the Group makes annual lease payments for leasehold land, that the right-of-use sits within investment properties, and that there are no externally imposed covenants.

Read cold, "3-year lease from 1 November 2023" describes a short-dated asset. It is the third term of a thirteen-year lease. That is not a small difference, and the disclosure has moved backwards: the reader of the FY2020 report was told more about this asset's tenure than the reader of the FY2025 report.

The landlord, and the zoning that explains the reversion

The 2014 grant was copied to JTC and to the Singapore Land Authority under land reference SLA/LPB/34.3.1MK7(G). Neither annual report names a lessor; the planning file does. Its condition (b) made retention of the structures on expiry a matter for JTC. For Pioneer Lodge the counterparty is different again — the FY2020 report refers to a sub-tenancy agreement with BCA.

And the reversion clause is not arbitrary. The Master Plan land use at 70 Tuas South Avenue 1 is Business 2 — industrial. The dormitory is a temporary use sitting on industrially zoned land, which is why the current permission provides that the premises are to be used for industrial or warehouse purposes once the temporary use ceases. The underlying zoning was never anything else.

The 2020 reduction to 11,808 was tied to compliance with the new requirements for workers' dormitories; occupancy against that reduced base was about 60 per cent at 31st March 2021. The count now stands at 15,744. Against condition 6 of the current permission — no increase in worker numbers on any renewal — how that ceiling is measured is a question the disclosure does not answer.

Two further conditions in the original are worth setting against the current one.

The reversion clause has softened — from demolition subject to JTC's requirement, to a mandated industrial or warehouse use. The renewal notice period has lengthened. And the land charge has changed name, the differential premium regime having since been superseded by Land Betterment Charge; a charge of that family has been payable on this site before, which is the context advisory note 7 sits in.

One more number. The 2014 permission approved capacity for 16,800 workers. The asset operates 15,744 beds — 1,056 fewer than approved. Under condition 6 of the current grant, that count cannot increase on renewal.

The permission and the lease term end on the same day. Three years from 1st November 2023 is 31st October 2026, which is also the cessation date in planning condition 3. The press release describes the lease as expiring "in November". The precise date is the last day of October.

Why the valuer is at 12 to 15 per cent

Knight Frank valued the workers' dormitories at S$194.08 million at 31st December 2025 on an income capitalisation approach at a capitalisation rate of 12 to 15 per cent, against a flat 15 per cent a year earlier (annual report Note 18, page 152). Residual value inputs in the same table give gross development value per bed of $13,810 and construction cost per bed of $8,727 for the prior year.

The comparison that isolates what is being priced sits four rows down the same table, in the same note, on the same balance sheet date:

Same company, same note, same year end. The gap is not a market judgement about dormitory demand, and it is not a country risk premium of nine points. It is tenure, and a use that reverts.

The group named the mechanism itself in the FY2025 Financial Review at page 15: valuation adjustments on the workers' dormitories "arising from lease decay due to the shortening of the remaining lease tenure", adding that the adjustments are accounting in nature and do not affect operating cash flows. Both halves of that are correct.

Note 18 at page 151 shows the arithmetic. The carrying amount is not the valuers' figure — it is that figure grossed up by the lease liability underneath it: S$152.58 million of valuation reports plus S$52.08 million of lease liabilities equals the S$204.66 million carried at the year end. Lease liabilities fell S$29.22 million across FY2025. As they amortise, the carrying value follows.

Over the same period rental income from investment properties rose, to S$92.76 million in FY2025 from S$84.61 million, and Tuas View held 95 per cent occupancy for 2025. The asset performed. The tenure shortened.

What the statutory commentary contains, and what it does not

Section 4 of the interim announcement's Appendix 7.2 disclosure is headed as a commentary on "any known factors or events that may affect the Group in the next operating period and the next 12 months". Its workers' dormitories paragraph at page 31 gives bed count, Dormitory Transition Scheme compliance, occupancy of 91.5 per cent for the half and a note on favourable rental rates. It does not mention that the tenure and the planning permission both end inside the next quarter.

The press release does, at page 2, and the results presentation does, at slide 13. So the fact is disclosed — in the two documents that carry no statutory obligation to carry it, and not in the one that does.

The half itself, briefly

Profit before tax rose S$3.13 million. Gross profit fell S$13.35 million and gross margin fell 10.67 percentage points, from 54.06 to 43.39 per cent. The improvement is the net of a S$45.45 million swing in other gains and losses against a S$28.85 million deeper fair value loss on investment properties — two non-operating lines, S$16.59 million of net movement between them, on a S$3.13 million result.

Within other gains and losses: realised derivative gains of S$12.00 million, fair value gains on financial assets of S$10.11 million, currency exchange gains of S$6.28 million and a S$9.40 million gain on the partial disposal of Lowood One. Foreign exchange and derivatives together contributed 23.6 per cent of profit before tax.

Two things improved that the reporting understates. Construction revenue rose 162.4 per cent to S$67.24 million with a segment result of S$9.09 million against a S$2.75 million loss. And beneath a fund management line down 93.3 per cent, recurring fee income rose 40.5 per cent — S$2.70 million this half against S$1.92 million of over-time revenue in the prior half, once the S$38.39 million performance fee is separated out.

What we are watching

The October update, on three counts rather than one. Whether the permission is renewed. Whether Land Betterment Charge is levied on that renewal, and at what quantum. And whether the worker-number cap in condition 6 carries into a renewed permission, because that determines whether the larger half of a 26,244-bed platform can grow again or is fixed at its present count.
