Weekly Landed Caveats, Week of 3 August 2026: 49 Landed Transactions Worth S$311.5 Million — Two Halves of One Subdivided Thomson Plot Set Consecutive District 20 Records Three Months Apart, While a Hillcrest Semi-Detached Clears S$4,505 Psf Against 1,198 Prior Sales

Forty-nine landed caveats across the 4th and 7th August releases, totalling S$311.5 million. Both halves of a subdivided plot on Old Upper Thomson Road set the District 20 record for a 99-year semi-detached house within three months of each other, and a…

By PropertyAtlas·10 August 2026·7 min read
Photo: Illustration: PropertyAtlas. The 30th July sale at 50 Old Upper Thomson Road at S$4.15 million is the highest price ever paid for a 99-year semi-detached house in District 20, breaking the record set three months earlier by the other half of the same subdivided plot. Source: URA REALIS, releases dated 4 and 7 August 2026.

Forty-nine landed caveats were lodged across the 4th and 7th August URA releases, totalling S$311.5 million. Thirty-four matched to a prior sale at the same address. Every one of them made money. Those thirty-four sellers had paid S$104.3 million for their houses; they sold them for S$186.3 million, a realised gain of S$82.0 million.

Two release dates, Tuesday 4th and Friday 7th August, are reported here as one week. Sale dates span 8th May to 31st July 2026. Fifteen districts are represented, and seven of the forty-nine are strata title; the rest hold land.

Price per square foot is reported separately for freehold and leasehold, because the two are not comparable. Freehold and 999-year titles accounted for 41 of the 49 caveats, ranging from S$664 to S$4,505 per square foot and averaging S$2,314. The eight leasehold transactions ranged from S$327 to S$2,063, averaging S$1,314. The week’s lowest figure, S$327 per square foot, is a terrace on a 99-year lease granted in 1947 with about twenty years left to run.

Land title and strata title are separated for the same reason, and the gap is wider than the tenure one. The 42 land-title houses ranged from S$327 to S$4,505 per square foot and averaged S$2,341, on S$283.3 million of value. The seven strata houses ranged from S$664 to S$1,257 and averaged S$1,010, on S$28.2 million. The strata figure is not a discount on the same thing: a strata rate is struck on the strata area of the house, while a land rate is struck on the land the house sits on, and the two measure different quantities. Cluster housing also carries shared land, a management corporation and monthly charges that a land-title house does not. Comparing the two rates directly will mislead in both directions.

Two halves of one plot, three months apart

In November 2022 a detached house at 50 Old Upper Thomson Road sold for S$3.40 million on 4,658.66 square feet of land, on a 99-year lease dating from 1995. That house has since been demolished — the No. 50 that transacted in July is a different building on part of the same land. Subdivision permission granted on 30th October 2025 split the plot into two, each carrying a three-storey semi-detached house with an attic.

Subdivision of a leasehold plot is the rare case. The economics normally only work on freehold land, where the developer is not spending on a title that is running down and buyers are not pricing in a lease that will need topping up. This plot had a 99-year term dating from August 1995, leaving roughly sixty-eight years at the point of subdivision — long enough for the arithmetic to hold, but the exception rather than the pattern.

The two halves have now both sold. The first, at 48 Old Upper Thomson Road on 2,239.99 square feet, transacted on 22nd April 2026 at S$4.00 million — the highest price ever paid for a 99-year semi-detached house in District 20, against 142 prior sales of 99-year semi-detached houses in the district. The second, retaining the parent address at 50 Old Upper Thomson Road on 2,418.67 square feet, sold on 30th July 2026 at S$4.15 million, breaking the record its own twin had set three months earlier.

The two child areas sum to 4,658.66 square feet. Exactly the parent, to the square foot.

PropertyAtlas does not pair either sale against the 2022 purchase of the parent plot. Half a plot measured against a whole one is not a like-for-like comparison, and the S$750,000 apparent gain that such a pairing would produce is meaningless. Both records carry a note on the platform recording the subdivision and naming the parent.

A District 11 rate record on new stock

A semi-detached house at Hillcrest Road sold on 22nd July for S$14.10 million on 3,130.17 square feet — S$4,505 per square foot, the highest ever paid for a freehold semi-detached house in District 11. The previous best was S$4,258, set on Kheam Hock Road in February 2025 at S$13.60 million on 3,193.68 square feet. The comparison runs against 1,198 prior sales of freehold semi-detached houses in District 11 since records began — not against detached houses, terraces, leasehold stock, or other districts. That is a deep denominator; this is not a record set on a thin sample.

The district’s record book is being rewritten quickly. The third-highest rate ever paid for a freehold semi-detached house in District 11 — S$3,945 per square foot on Greenwood Avenue — was set in last week’s release. Two of the top three have landed in consecutive weeks.

One qualification belongs with it. The house is newly built, and new stock commands a premium over a house in original condition. The 1,198 sales it is measured against are a mixed stock of new and old. The record is real; it is a record for the newest thing in the district.

Two further district records fell, both narrow. A freehold terrace on Pavilion View reached S$2,904 per square foot in District 23 against a prior high of S$2,812, across 933 prior sales of freehold terraces in that district. A 99-year terrace on Westwood Crescent reached S$1,626 in District 22 against S$1,622 — a margin of four dollars across 946 prior sales of 99-year terraces, which is a tie in everything but arithmetic.

Thirty-four pairs and not a single loss

Every matched pair this week came in at 0.00 per cent area variance, and every one gained. Zero losses in a set of thirty-four is unusual; last week’s comparable set carried two.

The two largest gains sit side by side in Watten Estate. A semi-detached house on Watten Park, bought in June 1998 for S$1.96 million, sold at S$11.10 million — a gain of S$9.14 million, or 466 per cent over 28.1 years. A second on Watten Close, bought in March 1996 for S$3.10 million, sold at S$11.88 million, up S$8.78 million or 283 per cent over 30.4 years. Compounded, those are 6.36 and 4.52 per cent a year.

The mix runs against the usual pattern. Terrace houses took twenty-one of the thirty-four pairs and 44.5 per cent of the realised gain; semi-detached took nine pairs and 42.0 per cent; detached took four pairs and 13.6 per cent. Semi-detached carried the highest median gain at S$2.78 million, ahead of detached at S$1.79 million — the reverse of most weeks, and a consequence of the two Watten Estate sales landing in the same release.

The shortest holds were a terrace on Pavilion View at 3.3 years and one on Westwood Drive at 3.0 years, both ordinary resales at 5.98 and 5.83 per cent a year.

The lease that ran out of road

The longest hold of the week returned the least. A terrace on Jalan Asas, bought in October 1995 for S$470,000, sold on 28th July for S$580,000 — a gain of S$110,000, or 23.4 per cent, over thirty years and nine months. That is 0.69 per cent a year, against 4.52 and 6.36 for the two Watten Estate houses held over comparable spans.

The difference is tenure. The Jalan Asas lease was granted in March 1947 for 99 years and has roughly twenty years left. Its S$327 per square foot is the lowest figure in the week by a wide margin, and it will keep falling. A freehold house compounds; a short lease amortises.

Launch purchases, again at the bottom

Two sub-sales at Belgravia Ace both came from launch purchases and both sit at the foot of the annualised table. One bought in January 2022 at S$4.282 million sold at S$5.10 million — 19.1 per cent over 4.5 years, or 3.95 per cent a year. Another bought in April 2022 at S$4.531 million sold at S$5.00 million — 10.4 per cent, or 2.39 per cent a year.

Both are recorded as sub-sales rather than resales. The pattern is the same one visible last week at The Seawind and on Bukit Way: buying at launch roughly halves the typical gain. All seven strata pairs this week gained, but the two launch purchases produced the weakest annualised returns in the entire set of thirty-four.

Data note

Source: URA REALIS caveat data, releases dated 4th and 7th August 2026. House numbers are masked on all landed transactions. Dates are caveat lodgement dates and follow the transaction, typically by one to two weeks. Profit and loss is derived by matching each sale to the most recent prior transaction at the same address, computed on transacted price before costs, taxes and financing. Where paired areas differ by more than five per cent the comparison is not like-for-like and is flagged rather than reported; the 50 Old Upper Thomson Road sale is excluded from pairing on this basis, its land area being 48.1 per cent of the parent plot. Subdivision is verified against URA written permission records, decision P021025-16C1-Z000 in this instance. Holdings of three years or less may involve redevelopment, in which case the cost base is incomplete. Record comparisons are published at district level only, matched on property type and tenure, and measured against the full transaction history. One caveat lodged in an earlier release was withdrawn from the 7th August extract and has been marked aborted rather than deleted. This is descriptive market commentary, not investment, valuation or transactional advice.
