Weekly Landed Caveats — Releases of 18th and 21st August 2026: A Luxus Hill Drive Terrace Struck S$3,239 Per Square Foot, the Highest Rate District 28 Has Recorded, and All Five of the District’s Highest Rates Were Set This Year; 38 Caveats, S$258.14M

A terrace on Luxus Hill Drive sold at S$3,239 per square foot of land, the highest rate District 28 has ever recorded for a landed home — and the fifth of the district’s five highest rates to be set this year. Before 2026, none had passed S$3,000.…

By PropertyAtlas·24 August 2026·11 min read
Photo: Illustration: PropertyAtlas. A 999-year terrace on Luxus Hill Drive, caveated on 6th August at S$5,228,888 on 1,614.63 square feet, struck S$3,239 per square foot of land — the highest rate recorded for a landed home in District 28 across 6,274 prior sales. All five of the district’s highest recorded rates were set in 2026; before this year none had passed S$3,000 per square foot. Source: URA REALIS, releases dated 18th and 21st August 2026.

Thirty-eight landed caveats appeared in the URA REALIS releases dated 18th and 21st August 2026, totalling S$258.14 million. The caveats themselves were lodged between 14th July and 15th August — a caveat is lodged once a sale is agreed, and appears in a REALIS release some weeks later.

Twenty-four of them matched to a prior sale at the same address. Every one of the twenty-four sold for more than was paid for it — a realised gain of S$82.05 million, an average of S$3.42 million a house. There was no loss anywhere in the set.

Two releases, Tuesday 18th and Friday 21st August, are reported here as one week. Thirteen districts are represented. Thirty-seven of the thirty-eight hold land; one is strata title. Thirty-six were resales and two were new sales, both terraces at Pollen Collection II.

Price per square foot is reported separately for freehold and leasehold, because the two are not comparable. Freehold and 999-year titles accounted for 31 of the 37 land-title caveats, ranging from S$940 to S$3,239 per square foot and averaging S$2,127 by value. The single strata sale, a terrace at Barossa Gardens on Pasir Panjang Road, struck S$1,375 per square foot of strata area — a different quantity from the land rates above, and not a discount on the same thing.

Before this year, no District 28 house had passed S$3,000 a square foot

A terrace on Luxus Hill Drive caveated on 6th August at S$5,228,888 on 1,614.63 square feet — S$3,239 per square foot. Across the 6,274 landed sales District 28 has on record, not one has been struck at a higher rate.

Four of those 6,274 had previously crossed S$3,000 per square foot. All four were caveated this year — one in January, one in June, two in July — and all four sit within a few streets of one another in the north of the district: two on Luxus Hill Drive, one on Luxus Hill View, one on Seletar Green View. Before 2026, no landed home in District 28 had ever sold above S$3,000 per square foot of land. The district's five highest recorded rates have all been set in the past eight months.

Every one of the five was struck on an identical 1,614.6 square feet. That is the tell: this is not a district re-rating, it is one tightly defined 999-year terrace product, on near-identical plots across a handful of adjoining streets, being repriced in a series of steps — S$3,041 in January, S$3,232 in June, S$3,202 and S$3,151 in July, and S$3,239 in August.

The record broken on 6th August was set on 11th June, on the same street, at S$3,232. The August sale clears it by seven dollars a square foot, and clears the highest price previously paid for a terrace in the same vicinity, S$5,218,888, by S$10,000. Two records, both real, both decided by rounding error — which is what a record looks like when the level has been tested repeatedly and stepped fractionally past, rather than jumped.

Set against the district as a whole, the gap is wide. District 28 has recorded 405 terrace sales since the start of 2024, averaging S$1,923 per square foot of land. The August sale is 68.4 per cent above that average. The vicinity is not catching up to District 28; it has been trading clear of it, and this sale is the top of a band it already occupied.

The reason sits in the product rather than the postcode. District 28 spans a mixed stock — Seletar Hills, Jalan Jarak, Mimosa, Sunrise Terrace, Pollen Collection — freehold and leasehold, old and new, terrace and detached, on plots of every size. The streets setting these rates are the opposite: uniform 999-year terraces on the same plot area, released in phases. A rate struck there says something precise about one product. Measured against the district it says something looser, and the distance between S$3,239 and S$1,923 is that difference, not a market-wide move.

New leasehold at nearly three times old leasehold

The six leasehold transactions split cleanly in two, and the split matters more than the range. Four were resales of older houses, from S$716 to S$988 per square foot, averaging S$897. The other two were new sales at Pollen Collection II, at S$2,609 and S$2,615 — 2.9 times the rate the four resales achieved.

The gap is not only in the rate. Those two new houses were also the most expensive leasehold homes in the batch by quantum, at S$4.21 million and S$4.22 million, on plots of 1,614.6 square feet — roughly half the land of the four resales, which averaged 3,288 square feet and none of which passed S$3.25 million. The leases are barely comparable either: the new houses run 99 years from April 2025, while the resales carry leases granted between 1995 and 2010.

Set against the freehold side of the same releases, the two new leasehold houses look less like an exception. They came in 22.8 per cent above the S$2,127 weighted average struck by the 31 freehold and 999-year land-title caveats, and 22 of those 31 — better than seven in ten — sold at a lower rate per square foot than a 99-year house at Pollen Collection II.

“Tenure used to be the first question a landed buyer asked, and it settled the price on its own. It no longer does,” said Tony Koe, founder of TK Real Estate and PropertyAtlas.sg. “Two 99-year terraces at Pollen Collection II caveated at around S$2,610 per square foot of land this week — close to three times what the four leasehold resales in the same releases achieved, and more than seven in ten of the freehold and 999-year houses managed. What buyers are paying for is a new house on an efficient plot. The title is now the second question, not the first.”

Quoting a single leasehold average across the two obscures all of it.

Twenty-four pairs and no losses

A clean sweep is unusual but not extraordinary. Last week thirty-three of thirty-four pairs gained; this week twenty-four of twenty-four did. What the run reflects is the length of the holds rather than the strength of the market — most of the twenty-four had been owned for a decade or more.

The four strongest annualised returns are recorded. A semi-detached house on Jalan Jarak in Seletar Hills Estate returned 18.41 per cent a year over 5.4 years; a terrace on Elizabeth Drive in Hillview Estate 16.47 per cent over 5.8 years; a terrace on Everitt Road 13.47 per cent over 19.2 years; and a semi-detached house on Jalan Rabu in Thomson Garden Estate 11.03 per cent over 9.1 years.

The Everitt Road pair is the largest multiple in the batch. The house last changed hands in May 2007 at S$475,000 on 1,756.68 square feet, and caveated on 11th August at S$5.40 million — a gain of S$4.925 million, or 1,036.8 per cent, compounding at 13.48 per cent a year over 19.2 years. The recorded land area is identical across three legs going back to 1997, so no subdivision or amalgamation sits between them.

The 2007 leg is the oddity, and District 15's own history explains it. At S$475,000 the seller paid S$270 per square foot — below the S$518 the same house fetched in January 1997, a decade earlier. That inversion looks like an error until the year is put in context.

District 15 recorded 424 freehold terrace sales in 2007, the busiest year of its decade, at a median of S$610 per square foot. But 87 of them — better than one in five — went below S$500, and the cheapest changed hands at S$204. A rate of S$270 sat in the bottom tail of a busy market, not outside it.

What followed removed that tail. The district's median freehold terrace rate rose to S$717 in 2008, S$882 in 2010 and S$1,118 by 2011. Sales below S$500 per square foot fell from 87 in 2007 to eleven in 2008, one in 2010, and none at all in 2011. The financial crisis of 2008 shows up in this district as a collapse in volume — 424 sales to 166, a fall of 61 per cent — rather than in price, which kept rising through it. The buyer of May 2007 caught the last of a price band District 15 has not offered since.

Nineteen years later the same house sold at S$3,074 per square foot.

Records set against real history, and one set against three sales

A terrace on Elizabeth Drive in Hillview Estate took District 23 at S$2,279 per square foot against 1,263 earlier district sales, and S$8,688,888 in price. It also took the Hillview Estate record, though the estate has only three earlier sales of its own — too short a history to carry much weight. A semi-detached house on Jalan Jarak reached S$2,361 per square foot against a prior street high of S$1,977, across 113 recorded street sales.

The largest single quantum of the week was a freehold detached house on Jalan Harom Setangkai in Chip Hock Gardens at S$24.28 million on 9,270.02 square feet, or S$2,619 per square foot. It is the highest price recorded on that street: the previous high, S$22.0 million in February this year, bought a larger plot of 10,800.6 square feet at a lower rate of S$2,037 per square foot.

What the pairing does and does not say

Two of this week's largest gains need a qualification the arithmetic will not supply.

A freehold detached house on Gentle Road is the second-largest transaction of the week, caveated on 11th August at S$18.50 million on 7,660.66 square feet in District 11. It last sold in June 2008 at S$6.50 million — up S$12.0 million, or 184.6 per cent, over 18.1 years, compounding at 5.94 per cent a year.

A house was built between the two legs. URA granted written permission in 2009, amended in March 2011, for a two-storey detached dwelling with a basement and an attic across two amalgamated lots. The 184.6 per cent is therefore not a land return. Construction cost does not appear in REALIS and is not in the figure. The two-lot amalgamation also explains a 3.90 per cent difference in recorded land area between the legs — inside the five per cent tolerance, flagged rather than suppressed, and flagged again here.

A detached house on Berrima Road in Dunearn Estate caveated on 7th August at S$14.88 million on 5,304.50 square feet, S$2,805 per square foot. It is paired against a November 2006 purchase at S$5.60 million on an identical area — up S$9.28 million, or 165.7 per cent, at 5.09 per cent a year over 19.7 years.

Where the money went

District 11 led on value with S$61.16 million across five caveats — 23.7 per cent of the week's total from 13 per cent of its transactions — carrying Gentle Road, both Hillcrest Road sales, Berrima Road and Watten Rise. District 19 followed with S$40.75 million across seven, and District 28 led on volume with eight caveats worth S$37.21 million.

The concentration is worth stating plainly: the five largest transactions carry 34.0 per cent of the S$258.14 million, and the three largest carry 23.0 per cent.

Detached houses drove it. Six detached sales — 15.8 per cent of the transactions — accounted for 36.1 per cent of the value, at a weighted average of S$2,270 per square foot of land. That is above the S$1,963 weighted average struck by the twenty-two terraces, which inverts the usual relationship in this data, where the smallest plots carry the highest land rates. On six transactions the inversion describes this week's mix, not a change in the pattern.

Data note

Source: URA REALIS caveat data, releases dated 18th and 21st August 2026. House numbers are masked on all landed transactions. Dates shown in the table are caveat lodgement dates, not release dates: a caveat is lodged once a sale is agreed and appears in a REALIS release some weeks later. The thirty-eight caveats in this batch were lodged between 14th July and 15th August 2026 and released on 18th and 21st August. Profit and loss is derived by matching each sale to the most recent prior transaction at the same address, computed on transacted price before costs, taxes and financing. New sales are not used as a sell leg. Where paired areas differ by more than five per cent the comparison is not like-for-like and is flagged rather than reported; the Gentle Road pair carries a 3.90 per cent area difference arising from a two-lot amalgamation and is reported with that flag. Sales of newly built houses on subdivided or redeveloped plots are not paired against the purchase of the parent plot; a February 2000 transaction at the Berrima Road address is excluded from pairing on this basis, its land area being 3.02 times the plot sold this month. Subdivision and redevelopment are verified against URA written permission records. Record comparisons are struck at four grains — district, postal sector, enclave and street — against prior transactions of the same property type, and where the comparison set is small the sample size is stated. Land areas are as recorded in the caveat.
