OUE Limited's Revenue Rose 5.3 Per Cent And Its Loss Reached S$114.6 Million. A Development Properties Segment With S$138,000 Of Revenue Carried S$107.7 Million Of It, And S$593.8 Million Is Still Carried Against An Associate That Has Not Been Profitable Since 2024

OUE Limited's revenue rose 5.3 per cent to S$308.3 million while the loss attributable to shareholders reached S$114.6 million. A Development Properties segment that earned S$138,000 in the half produced S$107.7 million of losses, and everything else in…

By PropertyAtlas·19 August 2026·11 min read
Photo: Hilton Singapore Orchard on Orchard Road, the largest Hilton hotel in Asia Pacific at 1,080 rooms and valued at S$1,273.0 million at 31st December 2025. The hotel sits in OUE REIT's portfolio and is managed by OUE Limited as master lessee. Higher revenue per available room here and at Crowne Plaza Changi Airport lifted the Hospitality division's revenue 10.4 per cent to S$109.5 million in the first half of 2026 — one of the operating businesses that grew while a Development Properties segment earning S$138,000 produced S$107.7 million of losses. Credit: OUE Limited.

OUE Limited reported revenue of S$308.3 million for the six months to 30th June 2026, up 5.3 per cent, and a loss attributable to shareholders of S$114.6 million against a profit of S$35.6 million a year earlier. Every operating segment but one grew. The one that did not earned S$138,000 in the half, and produced S$107.7 million of losses.

The group’s own framing is that the loss is largely non-cash and does not threaten operations. On the cash evidence that is correct, and this article says so plainly. What changed materially in the half is somewhere else, and it is not in the release.

The Arithmetic of a Single Segment

OUE reports three strategic segments, with Real Estate split into three divisions. Development Properties is one of them. In the six months it recorded external revenue of S$138,000 — a hundred and thirty-eight thousand dollars, against group revenue of S$308.3 million.

Against that, it recorded a segment loss of S$60.745 million. Segment profit is defined in the accounts as profit before interest, tax and other gains and losses, so that figure already carries the division’s S$60.589 million share of equity-accounted losses but excludes the impairment. Add the S$47.0 million impairment, which the segment note on page 25 attributes wholly to Development Properties, and the division accounts for S$107.7 million.

Group loss before tax was S$64.4 million. Everything other than Development Properties was therefore running at roughly S$43 million of pre-tax profit.

The operating businesses did what they were supposed to. Hospitality revenue rose 10.4 per cent to S$109.5 million on higher revenue per available room at Hilton Singapore Orchard and Crowne Plaza Changi Airport. Investment properties and fund management held at S$95.5 million. Healthcare held at S$75.4 million. The food and beverage business rose 20.8 per cent to S$27.8 million on a new outlet and a full period from those opened last year.

Geographically the split is starker still. Singapore produced S$260.2 million of the S$308.3 million, or 84.4 per cent. The People’s Republic of China produced S$2.4 million — eight-tenths of one per cent of revenue, and substantially all of the loss.

What Sits Inside Development Properties

The division holds OUE’s stake in Gemdale Properties and Investment Corporation Limited, the Hong Kong-listed PRC developer in which OUE is a material associate holder. Nothing much else of scale: the residual OUE Twin Peaks units, one of which was sold in the period for S$2.5 million with a second under option at S$3.8 million.

Note 6 on page 13 is the one to read, and it says something the press release does not. The impairment recognised against interests in associates now stands at S$67.0 million, against S$20.0 million at 31st December 2025. Twenty million was taken in FY2025. Forty-seven million was taken now. The release names the S$47.0 million and stops there; the cumulative figure appears only in the note.

The impairment indicators OUE discloses are not ambiguous. Gemdale’s business has been adversely affected by the sustained slowdown in the PRC property market. Its share price trades at a discount to net asset value per share. Several PRC developers have defaulted. And, in the company’s own words on page 14, Gemdale has not been profit-making since 2024.

The recoverable amount was assessed on a value-in-use basis, estimating future cash flows from Gemdale’s development properties and those of its own significant equity-accounted investees. The conclusion, from the same note: declining sales velocities and lower projected selling prices meant the carrying amount exceeded the estimated recoverable value.

What remains is the number worth carrying forward. Development Properties still holds equity-accounted investees of S$593.8 million, down from S$652.2 million at the year end. That is after S$67.0 million of cumulative impairment. Management states it will actively monitor the situation to assess the need for additional impairment charges — which is the correct disclosure, and also a plain statement that this is not necessarily finished.

The Non-Cash Defence Is Sound

The release says the loss is largely non-cash and that the group does not expect any material impact on operational cash flows or corporate funding requirements. Companies say this routinely and it is often worth testing. Here it tests well.

Cash generated from operations was S$114.2 million against S$109.7 million. After tax paid of S$15.7 million, net cash from operating activities was S$98.5 million, up 1.4 per cent. A group reporting a S$114.6 million attributable loss produced more operating cash than it did in the half when it reported a S$35.6 million profit. The S$47.0 million impairment, the S$2.9 million goodwill write-off and the S$53.2 million share of associate losses are all added straight back in the reconciliation, because none of them moved money.

Finance expenses also fell hard, down 25.0 per cent to S$64.8 million. Borrowing costs alone fell 13.7 per cent to S$52.5 million on lower interest rates, and the prior period carried an S$18.0 million foreign exchange loss — mostly a one-off realised loss on remitting the Lippo Plaza Shanghai sale proceeds — against S$6.0 million now.

So the operating business generated cash, the financing cost of that business fell, and the reported loss came from marks against an associate OUE does not control. That is a fair reading, and it is the company’s reading.

What Did Move

Cash and cash equivalents fell S$97.2 million to S$226.5 million. Borrowings due within twelve months rose from S$760.0 million to S$1,058.3 million. And unutilised committed facilities fell from S$577.5 million to S$430.3 million.

Those three lines move together, and the group discloses the consequence itself at item 9 on page 30: negative working capital of S$387.6 million, mainly due to the increase in current borrowings, with refinancing in progress.

Six months ago committed liquidity covered the next twelve months of maturities with room to spare. It no longer covers them. The cover ratio is a PropertyAtlas derivation from figures OUE discloses; the components are all the company’s own.

This is a refinancing observation and not a solvency one. OUE raised S$815.3 million of new borrowings in the half and repaid S$565.6 million, so the market is lending. In May it completed an inaugural seven-year green notes issue of S$150.0 million at a 3.25 per cent coupon, which it describes as its lowest-ever coupon and narrowest credit spread — a genuine achievement, and evidence that the credit is well received. It also holds S$101.8 million of capital commitments on Hotel Indigo Changi Airport.

But the direction of travel over six months is one-way, and the group’s gearing was already 53 per cent at the FY2025 year end, against 47 per cent a year before that. The interim announcement does not disclose a gearing ratio.

The Hotel Surplus Grew Because the Hotels Got Older

Note 3 on page 11 records that OUE’s hotel properties carry an open market value of S$1,784.0 million against a net book value of S$1,618.7 million, leaving a surplus of S$165.3 million that has not been incorporated in the financial statements. At 31st December 2025 the surplus was S$151.8 million.

An unrecognised surplus that grew S$13.5 million in a half sounds like an asset appreciating quietly behind a reported loss. It is not.

Both figures rest on the same valuation, taken as of 31st December 2025. The note says so. Net book value fell from S$1,632.2 million to S$1,618.7 million over the half — S$13.5 million of depreciation — and the surplus grew by exactly that amount. Nothing was revalued and nothing appreciated. The gap widened because the carrying value came down.

Investment properties were not revalued either. They are carried at the 31st December 2025 independent valuations adjusted for capital expenditure, lease incentive capitalisation and translation, with management’s internal assessment concluding fair value had not materially changed. That is orthodox for an interim, and it means the S$4.16 billion on the balance sheet is a December mark carried forward.

Where the Cash Actually Went

OUE paid S$7.5 million to its own shareholders in the half — the FY2025 final dividend of 1.0 cent. It paid S$57.1 million to non-controlling interests.

That is the structural fact about OUE which a consolidated income statement obscures. The group consolidates OUE REIT, in which it holds an effective 48.9 per cent interest, and First REIT through OUE Healthcare, in which it holds 89.68 per cent. Revenue and assets come in at 100 per cent; the cash goes out mostly to other people. Non-controlling interests took S$33.0 million of profit in a half when owners of the company took a S$114.6 million loss, and they hold S$1.91 billion of the group’s S$4.75 billion of equity.

The interim dividend is held at 1.0 Singapore cent, tax-exempt one-tier, payable on 30th September 2026 with books closing on 17th September. Against S$98.5 million of operating cash flow that is comfortably covered.

Two Things the Release Does Not Say

The release states that revenue rose on improved contributions across all business segments. Development Properties revenue fell 2.8 per cent, from S$142,000 to S$138,000. The quantum is trivial and the point is small, but the claim as written is not accurate, and it is the same division carrying the loss.

The second is a matter of sequencing rather than accuracy. The Business Review records that OUE Healthcare celebrated the official opening of Prince Bay Hospital in Shekou, Shenzhen on 24th July 2026, describing it as an important step towards becoming a leading healthcare destination in the Greater Bay Area. Note 6 records that during the same period OUE Healthcare granted an interest-free loan of S$10.2 million to the joint venture that operates it, taking total loans to S$13.7 million, and that the carrying amount of those loans was reduced to nil following recognition of S$13.7 million of the venture’s losses — S$8.3 million of which were losses incurred in 2025 that had not previously been recognised because they exceeded the group’s net investment.

Both are true. A flagship hospital opened, and the vehicle that operates it consumed the group’s entire net investment in it. Only one is in the release.

A further S$2.9 million of goodwill was written off against a healthcare cash-generating unit, in the note’s words amid the challenging operating environment in the PRC.

The Parts That Are Working

It would be a poor reading of this result to treat it as a company in trouble.

OUE REIT completed the acquisition of a 19.9 per cent interest in Salesforce Tower in Sydney on 16th March 2026 for S$176.5 million, funded from the Lippo Plaza Shanghai divestment proceeds — its first asset outside Singapore, and one of the two contributors the accounts name as offsetting associate losses. The other is OUE Allianz Bayfront LLP, on lower finance costs.

First REIT’s Indonesian divestment cleared its unitholder vote on 23rd June 2026, and S$467.4 million of assets sit as held for sale at the balance date against S$10.6 million of associated liabilities. Eight hospital properties go to PT Siloam for approximately S$389.2 million, two further properties to PT Lippo Karawaci for S$53.3 million, and the commercial rights to Lippo Plaza Kupang for S$29.1 million. Completion is expected within six months of approval, and the stated intent is to repay the majority of First REIT’s debt. On 14th July, after the balance date, First REIT hedged approximately 45 per cent of its Indonesian investment properties with S$125 million of non-deliverable forwards.

In June OUE agreed to acquire Crowne Plaza Changi Airport from OUE REIT in a joint venture with Tokyo Century — the second collaboration with that partner after the Hotel Indigo Changi Airport development, and consistent with the stated asset-right strategy of optimising capital deployment while growing third-party funds under management. Funds under management stand at S$7.6 billion across the two REIT platforms and managed accounts, against S$7.3 billion at the year end.

And the Singapore commercial and hospitality portfolio, which is what most readers of this newsroom actually care about, is performing. Singapore office rents rose 0.8 per cent in the second quarter on CBRE’s numbers with core CBD Grade A vacancy at a record low 3.3 per cent, and OUE’s own investment properties and fund management division turned S$95.4 million of revenue into S$68.1 million of segment profit.

What to Watch

The S$593.8 million. That is what Development Properties still carries in equity-accounted investees after S$67.0 million of cumulative impairment, against an associate that has not been profitable since 2024 and whose recoverable amount rests on projections of PRC development sales velocities and selling prices. Management has said it will monitor for further charges. The FY2026 full-year accounts, with a fresh external valuation cycle behind them, are where that gets tested.

The refinancing. S$1,058.3 million falls due within twelve months against S$656.8 million of cash and committed undrawn facilities. OUE says it is in the process of refinancing and the evidence is that lenders are willing. The question is what it costs, and how much committed headroom is left when it is done.

Whether the Indonesian completion lands. S$467.4 million of held-for-sale assets, a majority-debt-repayment intent, and a currency that hit a historic low during the period.

And the arithmetic of the three years. OUE lost S$286.8 million attributable in FY2024 and S$220.9 million in FY2025, and has now lost S$114.6 million in a half. Net asset value per share has gone from S$4.41 at the end of FY2021 to S$3.78. Across that span the Singapore business has been broadly fine and a Chinese associate has not. At some point the question stops being how much the associate is worth, and becomes what the group intends to do about holding it.
