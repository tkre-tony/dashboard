Second Caveat At Sky Eden@Bedok Clears S$5,000 Per Square Foot — District 16 Retail Had Not Reached That Level Since 2015 At East Village, Where Every Launch Buyer Who Has Sold Since 2017 Has Lost Money

A second caveat at Sky Eden@Bedok has cleared S$5,000 per square foot, after the first in May put District 16 retail at that level for the first time since June 2015. The only other project to reach it is East Village, whose 2012 launch set the mark — and…

By PropertyAtlas·11 September 2026·12 min read
Illustration: PropertyAtlas. Two Sky Eden@Bedok retail caveats, at S$5,500 psf in May and S$5,254 psf on 26th August 2026, are District 16’s only retail caveats at or above S$5,000 psf since East Village’s in June 2015. Source: URA REALIS, releases dated 8th and 11th September 2026.

Forty-two commercial and industrial caveats were released across the 8th and 11th September 2026 URA REALIS updates, worth S$551.52 million. One of them is the second caveat at Sky Eden@Bedok above S$5,000 per square foot. District 16 retail had not reached that level since June 2015, and the only other project ever to reach it, East Village, has not produced a profitable resale by a launch buyer since 2017.

S$457.56 million of the week is a second caveat on CapitaLand Ascendas REIT’s acquisition of 25 Loyang Crescent, not a new deal. Without it, the week comes to S$93.96 million across 41 caveats.

A second Sky Eden@Bedok caveat above S$5,000 psf

This week’s caveat at Sky Eden@Bedok, the mixed-use development at 1 Bedok Central, is the second there above S$5,000 psf: a 1,313 sq ft ground-floor unit at S$6.9 million, or S$5,254 psf, dated 26th August 2026. The first, in May, was a 775 sq ft ground-floor unit at S$4.26 million, or S$5,500 psf. Both are sub sales, on a 99-year lease from January 2022.

URA caveat data records 200 District 16 retail caveats since 1995. Before May, the last one at or above S$5,000 psf was dated 18th June 2015. Only one other project has ever reached that level: East Village, at 430 Upper Changi Road, whose April 2012 launch was the first time District 16 retail crossed it. Between April 2012 and June 2015, 51 East Village caveats were lodged at between S$5,080 and S$6,615 psf.

The buyers who sold early did well. All eight East Village resales between 2012 and 2015 were gains, one of them within three weeks of purchase. Every launch buyer who has sold since 2017 has lost money: 12 out of 12, by between 31 and 53 per cent, a median of 40.6 per cent and S$4.81 million in total. Four of those sales came in the past 14 months. The latest, on 24th August 2026, was a unit bought at S$5,280 psf in April 2012 and sold at S$3,622 psf — a loss of S$303,377, or 31.4 per cent, after 14 years.

The two projects differ in tenure. East Village is freehold; Sky Eden is on a 99-year lease. Sky Eden’s two sub sales, at S$5,254 and S$5,500 psf, sit inside the S$4,735 to S$5,956 psf range that East Village’s launch buyers paid for the units since resold — and 45 to 78 per cent above the S$3,097 to S$3,622 psf at which East Village units have resold in the past 14 months.

Shops inside new homes

Two developers sold retail units within residential projects this week. At Artisan 8, the freehold Sin Ming Road block of 34 apartments over eight ground-floor shops, a 280 sq ft shop sold for S$1.23 million, or S$4,395 psf. That is S$92,000, or 8.1 per cent, more than an identical 280 sq ft shop in the block fetched in October 2025. It is the fourth of the eight shops to be caveated, and all four have cleared S$3,700 psf, against the starting price of S$3,400 psf reported by EdgeProp when the project was previewed in August 2025.

At Chuan Park on Lorong Chuan, a 916-home development on a 99-year lease with two commercial units, according to its marketing materials, a 1,119 sq ft basement unit sold from the developer for S$4.47 million, or S$3,996 psf. It is the first of the two to be caveated.

25 Loyang Crescent: a second caveat, not a second deal

The week’s largest caveat, S$457.56 million for 25 Loyang Crescent, is not a new transaction. It is the second caveat on CapitaLand Ascendas REIT’s acquisition of the Loyang Offshore Supply Base, announced on 24th March 2026 at a purchase consideration of S$504.2 million, which includes an upfront land premium of S$46.35 million. The REIT’s 1H 2026 results presentation in August listed the acquisition with completion expected in the third quarter.

The first caveat, dated 24th March 2026, recorded S$455.0 million for 3,428,885 sq ft of land. The new one, dated 26th August 2026, records S$457.56 million for 3,569,167 sq ft — 4.1 per cent more land for 0.6 per cent more consideration. The new figure sits close to the purchase consideration less the land premium, S$457.85 million. URA’s data carries both caveats; on PropertyAtlas the August caveat shows the March one as its previous caveat rather than as a purchase. It is not counted in this week’s resale gains and losses.

Two adjacent shophouses on Joo Chiat Road

Two adjacent freehold shophouses on Joo Chiat Road were caveated on 31st August 2026: one at S$10.08 million on 1,886 sq ft of land (S$5,345 psf), the other at S$10.0 million on 1,510 sq ft (S$6,622 psf). Neither has an earlier caveat in URA caveat data since 1995.

The resale book

Twenty-four of the week’s caveats have an earlier sale of the same unit on record. Sixteen are gains and eight are losses, a net S$8.96 million. One pair, 30 Woodlands Terrace, spans a lease re-grant — a new 30-year lease from February 2024 — so its S$700,000 gain is not like-for-like.

The largest gain in dollars is a Kampong Glam conservation shophouse on Arab Street, bought in July 2015 for S$3.9 million and sold for S$6.6 million, up S$2.7 million, or 69.2 per cent. The biggest percentage gain is at Henderson Industrial Park, where a unit bought for S$600,000 in March 2006 sold for S$2.97 million, up 395 per cent — nearly five times what its owner paid, 20 years on. The quickest is at Thomson Plaza: a 398 sq ft retail unit bought for S$1.7 million in March 2025 and sold for S$2.7 million 17 months later.

The largest loss is at Liner, 1 Tuas Bay Close, where a 12,239 sq ft unit bought for S$4.61 million in September 2022 sold for S$3.375 million, a loss of S$1.235 million, or 26.8 per cent, in under four years. The recorded area is 2.1 per cent smaller than at purchase, within the range treated as the same unit. At City Gate on Beach Road, a basement retail unit bought new for S$1.448 million in 2019 sold for S$838,000, down 42.1 per cent.
