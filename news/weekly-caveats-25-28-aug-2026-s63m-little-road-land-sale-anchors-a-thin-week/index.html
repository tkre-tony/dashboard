Weekly URA REALIS Updates — Weeks of 25th and 28th August 2026: A S$63.00 Million Freehold Industrial Land Sale at Little Road Anchors a Thin Week, While Lucky Plaza and Peninsula Plaza Run at Their Strongest Year-to-Date Since 2020; 48 Caveats, S$141.75M

Forty-eight caveats across the 25th and 28th August releases, worth S$141.75 million — of which one freehold industrial land sale at Little Road accounts for S$63.00 million. The other forty-seven come to S$78.75 million between them, twenty-seven of those…

By PropertyAtlas·28 August 2026·12 min read
Photo: Illustration: PropertyAtlas. One freehold industrial land caveat at Little Road took S$63.00 million of the S$141.75 million week; the other forty-seven caveats came to S$78.75 million. Source: URA REALIS, releases dated 25th and 28th August 2026.

Forty-eight commercial and industrial caveats were released across the 25th and 28th August 2026 URA REALIS updates, worth S$141.75 million between them. One of them is S$63.00 million. Strip out the freehold industrial land sale at Little Road and the remaining forty-seven caveats come to S$78.75 million — twenty-seven of those under S$1 million. This was a quiet week with one large trade sitting on top of it.

Away from the anchor, the more durable finding is at two ageing strata blocks on the central retail spine. Lucky Plaza has logged fourteen caveats so far this year against four by the same point in 2025; Peninsula Plaza is running at its second-strongest year-to-date since 2020. Each contributed one caveat to this week.

A thin week with one large trade in it

The largest caveat is XX Little Road — Lian Cheong Industrial Building — at S$63,000,000 for 25,694.74 sq ft of land, or S$2,452 psf. It is freehold, and it is a whole-building industrial land sale rather than a strata unit. The next largest caveat in the batch is S$11.40 million, a five-unit office amalgamation at ARC 380.

Set Little Road aside and the shape of the week is modest: S$78.75 million across forty-seven caveats, twenty-seven of them below S$1 million, and only one other trade above S$10 million. That is the ordinary run-rate of strata industrial turnover rather than a busy week, and the S$141.75 million headline should be read with that in mind.

Little Road also carries no prior caveat in the PropertyAtlas transaction record, so it does not enter the profit-and-loss set at all. Any reading of this week that leans on the twenty-eight paired trades is reading a set that excludes the single largest transaction in it.

The figure to interrogate is the S$2,452 psf rather than the S$63 million. It sits well above the S$510 median psf for strata multiple-user factory space this week, but the two are not comparable measures: one is land area on a freehold whole-building sale, the other strata floor area on leasehold units. The batch contains no other freehold industrial land sale to set it against.

Two central strata blocks are having their best year since 2020

Two of this week’s caveats sit in ageing strata blocks on the central retail spine. Both buildings are running well ahead of their recent history, and for different reasons.

Lucky Plaza, 304 Orchard Road

Freehold, and overwhelmingly strata retail: forty-one of its forty-six caveats since 2020 are retail rather than office. Fourteen caveats have been lodged to 19th August 2026 — double the seven recorded across the whole of 2025, and 2.8 times the 2020–2025 year-to-date average of four. Value follows: S$38.10 million so far this year against S$13.25 million for all of 2025.

Median retail PSF looks to have fallen sharply — S$5,212 this year against S$8,120 in 2025, down 36 per cent. That is a floor-mix effect rather than a repricing. Level 5 accounts for 23 per cent of 2026 retail caveats against 3.6 per cent across 2020–2025, and level 5 carries the lowest pooled median in the building at S$3,868. Level 1, whose pooled median is S$15,242, has not transacted at all this year, and basement caveats have fallen from a quarter of the record to 15 per cent. Buying has moved up the building and away from the ground and basement frontage, where PSF runs three to four times higher. Median unit size also rose, from 215 to 511 sq ft, but that is not the driver: across the building the correlation between unit area and PSF is 0.081, effectively nil.

Peninsula Plaza, 111 North Bridge Road

A 999-year leasehold block holding both strata office and strata retail, and the two behave differently. Nine caveats to 19th August is the second-strongest year-to-date since 2020, behind the twelve of 2024 and above the six-year average of 7.7. Value is S$19.04 million against S$27.29 million for the whole of 2025.

Retail median PSF is S$3,598 this year, the highest in the 2020-onward record, narrowly ahead of the S$3,586 of 2023. Office median is S$2,510, down 6.6 per cent from S$2,687 in 2025. Firm retail against easing office, in a single address. Only three retail caveats support that median, so it is a direction rather than an established trend.

This week’s two entries are Peninsula Plaza #04-XX, 17th July, S$1,950,000 at S$4,213 psf, and Lucky Plaza #05-XX, 17th August, S$2,890,800 at S$4,403 psf.

Every loss this week was industrial and western

Twenty-eight of the forty-eight caveats matched to a prior sale of the same unit. Twenty-five resold above their last price and three below. All three losses are industrial and all three sit in the West Region.

The two Westlink One lines should not be read as independent evidence. Both are adjoining warehouse units on the same floor, bought on the same day in October 2012 at the same price and sold on the same day in August 2026 at the same price, held 13.88 years. Caveat data does not identify buyers or sellers, so common ownership cannot be established from it — but two units moving on identical terms across fourteen years is one pattern rather than two. Realised losses total S$486,000 against S$7,790,364 of realised gains, a net of S$7,304,364 across the twenty-eight pairs.

Two further adjoining-unit pairs appear in the batch and distort any per-caveat superlative. Two units on the same floor at WCEGA Plaza were bought five weeks apart in mid-2009 and sold on the same day for gains of 172.7 and 173.7 per cent over roughly 17.2 years — the two highest returns of the week. Two units at Mandai Foodlink are identical on every recorded field: 2,540.30 sq ft each, S$791,700 each in January 2015, S$875,000 each this month. In each case the two lines move together, so the highest and the lowest returns of the week each rest on a single pattern rather than on two independent trades.

GATE+ supplies every new sale in the batch

Seven of the forty-eight caveats are New Sales and all seven are at GATE+, 9 Tukang Innovation Drive, across three sale dates in June and July at S$490 to S$600 psf. Every other caveat in the week is a Resale. PropertyAtlas covered the GATE+ launch caveats in the week of 17th July 2026; this is the continuing tail of that launch rather than a fresh event.

Twenty-nine years for 0.9 per cent

The longest hold in the batch is a Sim Lim Square retail unit at 1 Rochor Canal Road, bought in September 1997 for S$1,436,460 and sold on 14th August 2026 for S$1,449,988 — a gain of S$13,528, or 0.94 per cent, over 28.89 years. The same week contains a 3.01-year hold at A’Posh Bizhub returning 27.0 per cent and a 4.37-year hold at Woodlands Horizon returning 53.4 per cent. The largest gain by quantum is XX Woodlands Industrial Park E5, S$2,950,000 in March 2016 to S$4,950,000 this month: S$2,000,000, or 67.8 per cent, over 10.38 years.

Data note

Source: URA REALIS caveat data, releases dated 25th and 28th August 2026 — five and forty-three caveats respectively. All forty-eight were added to the PropertyAtlas transaction record, taking the commercial and industrial table to 83,026 rows. No date revisions and no price or area corrections were identified in this batch. Multi-year comparisons for Peninsula Plaza and Lucky Plaza draw on caveats lodged from 1st January 2020 onward and are normalised to 1st January – 19th August in each year.

Caveats are lodged after a sale is agreed and may be lodged well after the transaction date; sale dates in this batch run from 29th May to 19th August 2026. Caveats can be withdrawn and figures may be restated by URA in later releases. Profit and loss figures compare a unit against its own most recent prior caveat on the same address and area, are gross of stamp duty, agency fees, legal costs, financing and any capital expenditure, and are not adjusted for inflation. Twenty of the forty-eight caveats had no prior transaction on record and are excluded from the profit-and-loss set, including the S$63.00 million Little Road caveat.

This is a description of lodged caveat data. It is not a valuation, a forecast, or advice on any specific property.
