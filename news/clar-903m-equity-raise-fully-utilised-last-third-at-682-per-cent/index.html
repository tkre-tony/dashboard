CapitaLand Ascendas REIT Has Fully Utilised Its S$903.5 Million Equity Raise Five Months And Two Days After Pricing — The S$300.5 Million That Had Not Moved At 30th June Went Into Two Singapore Logistics Assets At A Blended 6.82 Per Cent, 136 Basis Points Above Everything Bought Before It

Three weeks ago PropertyAtlas asked whether the third of CapitaLand Ascendas REIT's equity raise that had not moved would convert, and recorded that the average yield on the whole raise depended on 25 Loyang Crescent landing. It landed on 27th August. The…

By PropertyAtlas·27 August 2026·9 min read
Photo: 25 Loyang Crescent, the 197,800 sqm logistics estate CapitaLand Ascendas REIT acquired from Toll Offshore Petroleum Services on a 13.4-year leaseback. Its completion on 27th August 2026 exhausted the March equity fund raising and, at 6.9 per cent, lifted the blended entry yield on the whole raise. Credit: CapitaLand Ascendas REIT.

CapitaLand Ascendas REIT announced on 27th August 2026 that the acquisition of 25 Loyang Crescent had completed, and that the gross proceeds of approximately S$903.5 million from the March 2026 equity fund raising have been fully utilised. Both statements occupy a single sentence each in a four-page filing.

They matter because the raise can now be measured. Reporting the first-half results on 6th August, PropertyAtlas recorded that S$603.0 million of the S$903.5 million had been deployed at 30th June, that S$300.5 million had not, and that the average yield on the entire raise depended on 25 Loyang Crescent landing, because at 6.9 per cent it was the highest-yielding use left in the allocation. That is no longer a question about the future. Every line of the allocation is now closed and every acquisition in it carries a yield the Manager has disclosed.

Yields are stated before and after transaction costs, as the Manager gives them. The blended figure is a PropertyAtlas calculation. It does not assume the equity funded each asset pro rata: the Manager’s own table allocates a stated number of dollars from the raise to each named acquisition, and those allocations are the weights. The equity share varies widely by deal — 60.1 per cent of the Canal Winchester cost, 30.0 per cent of Osaka. Against a weighted average all-in borrowing cost of 3.5 per cent, a post-transaction blended entry yield of 5.78 per cent is a spread of 228 basis points. That is the arithmetic of the raise, and on its own it is unremarkable. The interesting part is the order in which the money went out.

The last third carried the average

Split the deployment by whether the cash had moved at the half-year balance date and the raise separates into two quite different exercises.

The late tranche is S$300.5 million. That is not an approximation. It is precisely the balance of proceeds not yet used that the Manager reported in the first-half financial statements, and precisely the figure PropertyAtlas published on 6th August. It consists of exactly two assets: S$82.2 million for 5 Tuas Avenue 5 and S$218.3 million for 25 Loyang Crescent.

One qualification on timing. The 27th August filing lists an intervening use-of-proceeds announcement dated 18th August 2026, which is the probable date the 5 Tuas Avenue 5 tranche moved. PropertyAtlas has not read that announcement and does not assert the date.

The 136 basis point gap between the two tranches is real, and it is entirely responsible for the difference between a raise that averaged five-and-a-half per cent and one that averaged six. What it is not is evidence of improving discipline. The late tranche is two Singapore ramp-up logistics assets bought on long leasebacks. The early tranche carried a Japanese hyperscale data centre at 4.3 per cent. This is composition. The Manager did not get better at buying; it happened to buy the cheap-entry assets last.

Why this is not the 5.2 per cent PropertyAtlas published in August

Reporting the first half on 6th August, PropertyAtlas put the blended initial yield on the four completed acquisitions at approximately 5.2 per cent. That figure weighted the same four disclosed yields by total acquisition consideration — S$1,145.6 million of assets. The figures above weight them by each line’s slice of the equity raise. Both are recomputed from the same disclosed yields and neither supersedes the other; they answer different questions.

Consideration-weighting asks what the assets yield. Allocation-weighting asks what the equity bought, and it is the relevant question once the raise is closed and the Manager has attributed every dollar of it to a named use. The two diverge because the equity did not fund the deals evenly. Osaka is 54.2 per cent of the half’s completed acquisition spend but only 25.1 per cent of the equity deployed against it, so the lowest-yielding asset in the set weighs less heavily on the raise than it does on the portfolio.

Osaka is the price of the label

Osaka Data Centre 1 is 25.1 per cent of the equity-funded acquisitions and carries the lowest entry yield in the set. A 49 per cent interest cost S$627.6 million all-in against a S$1,299 million valuation on a 100 per cent basis, at 4.3 per cent before transaction costs, with a 13.8-year weighted average lease expiry and built-in annual rent escalation of 1.0 per cent.

25 Loyang Crescent cost S$536.9 million all-in against a S$518.0 million valuation, at 6.9 per cent, on a 13.4-year master lease to Toll Offshore Petroleum Services with built-in escalation of 2.5 per cent for the first twelve years.

Two assets of near-identical lease duration, 260 basis points apart on entry yield and 150 basis points apart on annual escalation. The data centre is the longer lease by five months and the weaker inflation hedge by a factor of two and a half. Neither is a criticism — hyperscale data centres price where they price, and the tenant is described as a global investment-grade hyperscaler. But the premium is now quantified in the issuer’s own disclosures rather than asserted.

The line that quietly ended

The March allocation contained a line reading “to partially finance the potential acquisition of a light industrial asset in Singapore and associated costs”, at S$30.4 million. In the revised table that line stands at zero, and a line that did not exist in March — debt repayment — stands at S$30.9 million.

PropertyAtlas reported on 6th August that roughly S$31 million of the raise had ended there. The composition is now exact. The S$30.4 million from the acquisition that was never made, plus S$0.5 million of fee underspend — S$10.7 million estimated against S$10.2 million actually incurred — comes to S$30.9 million. The reconciliation is complete to the dollar and the full table sums to S$903.5 million with no residual.

Roughly S$31 million out of S$903.5 million is 3.4 per cent, which is small. It is worth saying only because the deployment is otherwise so clean: one intended use in eight was abandoned, its money was disclosed as redirected, and the redirection was to the balance sheet rather than to another asset.

Five months and two days

The private placement was priced on 25th March 2026. The proceeds were declared fully utilised on 27th August 2026. That is five months and two days from pricing to full deployment of S$903.5 million across seven acquisitions in five countries, a fee line and a debt repayment.

For context and no more, CapitaLand Ascott Trust filed its own use-of-proceeds update the day before, on 26th August, showing S$66.0 million of a S$303.1 million raise still unspent three years after launch. The comparison is tempting and mostly unfair: Ascott’s residual is a live London renovation programme rather than idle capital, and a capex-funding raise does not deploy on the same clock as an acquisition-funding one. The two filings are worth reading together only as a reminder that “use of proceeds” covers two quite different disciplines.

The reason entry yields matter here rather than being a curiosity is visible in the distribution.

Distributable income rose 8.6 per cent. The unit base rose 8.5 per cent. Distribution per unit rose 0.1 per cent. The raise bought growth almost exactly equal to the dilution it created, which is what a blended entry yield of 5.78 per cent after transaction costs, against a 3.5 per cent cost of debt and an enlarged equity base, produces in its first partial period.

That is not a failure. Two of the seven acquisitions — the two highest-yielding after Canal Winchester, and together 40.1 per cent of the equity deployed — completed after the balance date and contributed nothing to the first half at all. The half being reported is the half that carried the dilution without the income. The test of the raise is the first full period in which 25 Loyang Crescent and 5 Tuas Avenue 5 are both earning.

Two completions cost more than a point of occupancy

Portfolio occupancy was 89.1 per cent at 30th June 2026, against 90.9 per cent at 31st December 2025 and 91.8 per cent a year earlier. The Manager states that excluding 27 IBP and Summerville Logistics Center, both completed in the second quarter, occupancy would have been 90.3 per cent.

The two are worth naming because they are the group’s own development output rather than acquisitions. 27 IBP in Singapore is a S$136.0 million redevelopment that doubled the gross floor area to 24,646 square metres and carries a Green Mark Platinum certification; it was 14.9 per cent occupied at 30th June. Summerville Logistics Center near Charleston cost approximately S$94.8 million, completed on 6th April 2026, and was 0.0 per cent occupied at 30th June with marketing described as in progress. Its expected initial yield of 7.6 per cent on a stabilised basis is the highest number anywhere in the group’s current investment set, and it is currently earning none of it.

Against 5,073,157 square metres of portfolio net lettable area, the vacant space in those two buildings is roughly 68,200 square metres, or about 1.3 per cent — which reconciles closely to the 1.2 percentage point difference the Manager quotes. The occupancy decline is, on the group’s own figures, substantially a story about two buildings that have just been handed over rather than about the leased estate.

Elsewhere in the Singapore book the softness is older and more stubborn. Acer Building stood at 6.4 per cent, down from 23.0 per cent at the year end. 17 Changi Business Park Central 1 was 36.4 per cent, 31 International Business Park 37.7 per cent, and 3 Changi Business Park Vista 51.1 per cent. Average rental reversion across the portfolio was nonetheless positive at 8.5 per cent for leases renewed in multi-tenant buildings, and the Manager expects reversions to stay positive in the high single digits for the full year.

Where the next money comes from

With the raise closed, the funding of the next tranche of activity is disclosed rather than inferred. In July the Manager announced the divestment of Kim Chuan Telecommunications Complex for S$200.4 million, against an independent valuation of S$151.8 million as at 30th June 2026 and an original purchase price of S$100.0 million. That is a 32.0 per cent premium to valuation and slightly more than double the entry cost, recomputed from the disclosed absolutes.

Five ongoing projects — two developments, a redevelopment and two asset enhancement initiatives — carry an aggregate investment of S$507.2 million and are scheduled for completion between 2026 and 2028. Aggregate leverage fell from 42.0 per cent at 31st March 2026 to 39.7 per cent at 30th June following the repayment of debt with raise proceeds; fixed-rate debt is 70.1 per cent of the total and the debt maturity profile is 2.5 years. The issuer rating is A3 from Moody’s. Portfolio assets under management stood at approximately S$20.1 billion across 234 properties, of which Singapore is 65 per cent, or S$13.1 billion.

One line PropertyAtlas cannot compute

The blended yields above exclude one item. S$113.6 million of the raise was allocated to the portfolio of 2 Pioneer Sector 1, Tuas Connection and 9 Kallang Sector. That portfolio completed on 30th December 2025, three months before the placement was priced, and no initial net property income yield for it appears in the results release, the financial statements, the supplementary information or the results presentation.

Because it completed before the raise existed, the equity refinanced it rather than funded it, which is a reasonable basis on which to leave it out of a calculation about what the raise bought. But the exclusion is a choice, it is stated here rather than buried, and a reader who wants the blend across the full S$862.4 million of acquisition lines will need a yield PropertyAtlas does not hold.
