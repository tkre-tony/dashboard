AUO's Former Display Plant On Tampines Industrial Avenue 3 Sold To Western Digital For S$350.0 Million, Below The S$400 Million And Then S$380 Million It Was Marketed At, Carrying 72.9 Per Cent Of A S$480.4 Million Week — While Buyers From 2015 To 2019 Sold At A Median Loss

AUO's former Singapore display plant carried nearly three-quarters of the week's value, sold to Western Digital at S$50 million below its first asking price. Behind it, buyers from before 2011 and after 2020 made money; buyers from 2015 to 2019 mostly did not.

By PropertyAtlas·2 October 2026·14 min read
PropertyAtlas figure card. Advanced Display Park on Tampines Industrial Avenue 3, AUO's former display plant, sold to Western Digital's Singapore unit for S$350.0 million, 12.5 per cent below the S$400 million first asked. Credit: PropertyAtlas analysis of URA REALIS caveat data and press reports.

The largest caveat released this week was Advanced Display Park on Tampines Industrial Avenue 3, a single-user factory in one of Singapore’s wafer fabrication parks, which sold on 10th September for S$350.0 million. That one caveat is 72.9 per cent of the S$480.4 million carried by the 60 caveats URA released on 29th September and 2nd October.

AUO’s Tampines plant, sold for S$50 million less than first asked

The building was the Singapore plant of AUO, the Taiwanese display maker, which closed production here at the end of 2023. AUO first marketed the facility in 2024 at S$400 million and lowered the asking price to an indicative S$380 million in April 2025, Mingtiandi and The Straits Times reported. The marketing agent, Cushman & Wakefield, described a six-storey building with a cleanroom ready for immediate use, a dual-source 50 megavolt-ampere power supply and about 34 years of land tenure, on a site of about 998,330 sq ft with a gross floor area of 1.91 million sq ft, EdgeProp reported.

On 2nd September 2026, AUO announced that it would sell the site to Western Digital’s Singapore unit for S$350 million before tax, with the disposal gain to be confirmed once the buyer secures local regulatory approvals, DigiTimes reported. TrendForce, citing AUO’s exchange filing and Commercial Times, put the price at about NT$8.753 billion for the facility and its related building facilities, and placed the sale alongside AUO’s disposals this year of its Huaya plant to Quanta for NT$19.7 billion and its Kaohsiung plant to ASE for NT$6.3 billion, about NT$34.7 billion of plant sales in 2026 as AUO shifts investment toward advanced packaging. The caveat, dated 10th September, records that price: 7.9 per cent below the April 2025 asking price and 12.5 per cent below the original S$400 million.

On the land area in the caveat, 997,340 sq ft, the price is S$351 per square foot of land; on the 1.91 million sq ft of floor area reported at marketing, roughly S$183 per square foot of gross floor area. The tenure in the caveat is 30 plus 28 years from 1st June 2001, which runs to 2059, about 32.7 years from the sale. It is the first caveat for the address in the PropertyAtlas database, so there is no earlier caveated price to measure the sale against.

The rest of the week: what thirty-eight paired resales show

Of the 60 caveats, 38 were for units with a prior caveat to measure against. Twenty-nine made money and nine lost. The median gain was 30.3 per cent over a median holding period of 11.8 years, about 3.1 per cent a year before stamp duty, legal costs, interest and agent fees. One further unit appears twice and is not counted; it is explained below.

Three single-user factories carry 79.0 per cent of the week’s value. Behind Advanced Display Park came JDE Building on Tuas Link 2 at S$15.0 million, also a first caveat for its address, and the Tuas View Square factory at S$14.38 million. Fifty-five of the 60 were strata and five were land. Sale dates run from 24th June to 25th September across 19 postal districts, with the Central Region taking 29.

GB Building: a full office floor on Cecil Street

The largest office deal of the week was the entire 11th floor of GB Building on Cecil Street, four units totalling 5,425 sq ft, sold on 22nd September for S$10.25 million, or S$1,889 per square foot. The building sits on a 99-year lease from October 1982, about 55 years remaining.

The same floor has changed hands three times before in the caveat record: for S$4.34 million in November 2006, S$7.16 million six months later in May 2007, and S$9.22 million in November 2019. Against the 2019 purchase, this sale is up 11.1 per cent over 6.8 years, about 1.6 per cent a year. Against the 2007 price, it is up 43.1 per cent over 19 years, about 1.9 per cent a year; the 2006 buyer, had they held, would be up 136 per cent.

Whole floors at GB Building trade often enough to give a running benchmark. The 8th and 26th floors sold in 2024 at about S$1,980 per square foot, and the 16th floor in April 2026 at S$1,880. At S$1,889, this week’s sale sits with the April deal and about 4.6 per cent below the 2024 level.

New sales, a removed caveat and a re-issued one

Five of the 60 were new sales. Three are basement retail units at Lentor Gardens Residences, each at S$1,180,650 or S$2,551 per square foot. One is an office unit at The Golden Mile on Beach Road, #10-XX, at S$5,301,700 or S$3,420 per square foot, on a 99-year lease from November 2024. The fifth is Space 18 at Lorong Ampas #03-XX, at S$2,594,023 or S$1,452 per square foot, freehold.

One caveat came off the register: Lian Cheong Industrial Building on Little Road, a S$63.0 million sale dated 12th August that appeared in an earlier release. It is no longer in URA’s data. On our side it is flagged as withdrawn rather than deleted, so it drops out of the totals and the profit-and-loss views while the audit trail remains. URA gives no reason, and no replacement caveat has appeared.

One caveat was re-issued. E-Centre @ Redhill on Jalan Bukit Merah, #03-XX, already carried a caveat for S$1,395,000 dated 26th August. This release adds a second caveat for the same unit, at the same price and area, dated 15th September. URA’s data now holds both, and so does ours. It is very likely the same transaction re-lodged with a new date rather than a second sale twenty days later, so it is not counted as a resale or a profit-and-loss pair above. If URA withdraws one of the two in a later release, it will be flagged then.
