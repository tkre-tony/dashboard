Weekly URA REALIS Caveats, Week of 3 August 2026: 60 Commercial and Industrial Transactions Worth S$170.3 Million — ESR-REIT Exits 12 Ang Mo Kio Street 65 at S$6.5 Million Below Its 2014 Cost While a Sims Lane Freehold Realises S$21.0 Million

Sixty caveats across the 4th and 7th August releases, worth S$170.3 million. Two whole-site industrial trades produced both the week's largest gain and its largest loss.

By PropertyAtlas·7 August 2026·9 min read
Photo: 12 Ang Mo Kio Street 65, divested by ESR-REIT in July 2026 at a 2.1 per cent premium to independent valuation and 16.3 per cent below the price paid in 2014. Credit: ESR-REIT.

Sixty commercial and industrial caveats were lodged across the 4th and 7th August URA releases, totalling S$170.3 million. That is the highest weekly figure since the start of July and 61 per cent above last week’s S$105.6 million. Almost all of the increase sits in two titles.

12 Ang Mo Kio Street 65 carries a caveat lodged on the 21st of July at S$33.3 million. A freehold whole-building on Sims Lane carries one lodged on the 24th at S$29.0 million. Together they account for S$62.3 million, or 37 per cent of the week, on two of the sixty caveats. Strip them out and the remaining fifty-eight are worth S$108.0 million — within three per cent of last week.

The two are worth reading together for a different reason. One produced the largest realised gain in the batch and the other the largest realised loss, and their caveats were lodged three days apart.

Industrial supplied forty-seven of the sixty caveats and 84 per cent of the value — an unusually lopsided split, and a direct consequence of the two land deals. Resale accounted for fifty-eight, new sale for two. By region: Central 32, West 13, North 10, North East 4, East 1. District 22 was busiest at twelve caveats, followed by District 14 at nine and District 27 at eight.

Six of the sixty were land or whole-building transactions, carrying S$92.9 million between them. The other fifty-four were strata.

Two whole sites, opposite outcomes

ESR-REIT sold 12 Ang Mo Kio Street 65 for S$33.3 million. The manager described the price as a 2.1 per cent premium to Edmund Tie’s independent valuation of S$32.6 million as at the 30th of June. Both figures are accurate, and both are less informative than the third one: Cambridge Industrial Trust — the vehicle that later became ESR-REIT — paid S$39.8 million for the same site in August 2014. The exit is S$6.5 million, or 16.3 per cent, below cost.

The reconciler is lease decay. At purchase the property carried roughly 36 years of remaining tenure. It now carries roughly 24. A premium to a current valuation and a discount to a twelve-year-old purchase price are not in conflict when the asset being valued has lost a third of its remaining life in between. What the caveat cannot show is the rental income collected across those twelve years, which is where the rest of the return, if there was one, would sit.

The caveat record adds a point the announcement does not. The 2014 caveat records tenure of 30 years from the 16th of October 1990; the 2026 caveat records 30 years from the 16th of October 2020. The lease was renewed during the holding period, which is what reconciles roughly 36 years of remaining term at purchase with roughly 24 now against a 2050 expiry. Whatever was paid to secure that second term is not recorded in caveat data and cannot be added to the S$39.8 million, so the S$6.5 million shortfall is measured against a cost base that is understated.

One framing caution. A REIT holds an asset for rental income, and the capital line is only one component of total return. Twelve years of rent from this property, the reversions achieved and the occupancy sustained across that period are not in caveat data and cannot be recovered from it. The figures above measure the change in capital value between two caveats and nothing else.

The Sims Lane building carries a caveat lodged three days later at S$29.0 million, against S$8.0 million on the caveat lodged in July 2010 — S$21.0 million, or 262.5 per cent, over 16.1 years. The obvious difference is tenure: the Sims Lane site is freehold and 12 Ang Mo Kio Street 65 is not.

One caution on the Sims Lane figure. The 2010 caveat records a land area of 12,020 square feet; the 2026 caveat records 10,182. Because the two areas differ by more than five per cent the pair is flagged as an area caveat rather than reported as a clean like-for-like.

Suntec Tower One: a matched floor plate

This week’s batch carries a Suntec Tower One level-9 unit of 3,928.86 square feet at S$2,275 per square foot — S$8.94 million, caveat lodged on the 21st of July.

The comparable is unusually clean. A unit of exactly 3,928.86 square feet on level 6 transacted in April 2019 at S$2,300 per square foot. Nearer in time, the adjacent level-9 unit went at S$2,900 in February 2023 and a level-8 unit at S$2,850 in July 2023.

The tower runs on a 99-year lease from March 1989, leaving about 62 years. Strata volume across the Suntec towers is thin in any given month, and a single print in one floor band is a data point rather than a direction.

Strata industrial: median down, distribution wide

Forty multi-user factory caveats carried a median of S$577 per square foot, against S$636 last week. The band is wide: CT Hub 2 at Lavender Street reached S$1,210 while Pioneer Lot at Benoi Road traded at S$88. Ubi and Woodlands were the busiest addresses, with three Harvest @ Woodlands units and two each at Ubi Techpark, Vertex, Mactech Building, Valiant Industrial Building and iSpace.

One week is not a trend and a median across a wide distribution moves easily on mix. The figure is reported here because it will matter if it repeats.

Ten of thirty-three pairs at a loss

Ten of thirty-three matched pairs realised a loss, against five of thirty last week. The ratio moved from 16.7 per cent to 30.3 per cent, on a sample small enough that it is reported as an observation and nothing more.

The largest was 12 Ang Mo Kio Street 65 at −16.3 per cent. Vision Exchange at Venture Drive also came in at −16.3 per cent, E-Centre @ Redhill at −13.8 per cent and Eco-Tech@Sunview at −35.4 per cent, the steepest by percentage. The remainder were single-digit declines on strata industrial.

The two shortest holds also lost: E9 Premium at Woodlands, bought June 2025 and sold thirteen months later at −4.7 per cent, and Woodlands Bizhub, bought May 2023 and sold at −6.4 per cent. Both are strata industrial in the same Woodlands cluster.

Elsewhere in the batch

Bukit Timah Shopping Centre produced the week’s highest rate at S$3,892 per square foot on a 398 square foot second-floor office unit, up 55 per cent on its May 2019 purchase. High Street Plaza returned the largest percentage gain of any strata unit at 248.4 per cent over 23.4 years, on an 86 square foot lot — the smallest area in the batch, where percentage figures should be read with that in mind.

Senoko Industrial Estate on Senoko Avenue recorded the lowest rate in the batch at S$72 per square foot across 55,485 square feet of land, and a 700 per cent gain against its September 2009 price. That figure needs its lease history attached. The 2009 caveat records tenure of 30 years from the 1st of September 1983, so the buyer took roughly four years of remaining term at S$9 per square foot. The current caveat records 18.83 years from the 1st of September 2013 — an extension granted after the original term expired, running to around July 2032 and leaving just under six years at the point of sale. The premium paid for that extension is not recorded in caveat data and cannot be added to the 2009 price, so the 700 per cent is a return on an undisclosed cost base rather than on S$500,000. The tenor is worth noting in itself: 18.83 years is a short, non-standard extension rather than a fresh thirty-year term.

The week’s only two new sales were both at GATE+ on Tukang Innovation Drive, at S$577 and S$515 per square foot on caveats dated the 6th of July. That takes the 265-unit, 33-year-leasehold ramp-up B2 development — a joint venture between SLB Development, Boustead Projects, KSH Holdings and Ho Lee Group — to 93 caveats worth S$107.8 million since its launch caveats first reached the register in the 17th July release. Both prints sit inside the S$490 to S$740 band established at launch and the project median holds at S$608. After 75 caveats in that first release and 11, 5 and now 2 in the three since, the register is reading as a steady tail rather than a second wave.

Data note

Source: URA REALIS caveat data, releases dated the 4th and 7th of August 2026. Dates shown are caveat dates, not completion dates. Unit numbers are masked to floor level and street numbers on land and whole-building transactions are masked.

One address is shown in full: 12 Ang Mo Kio Street 65, whose divestment was publicly announced by ESR-REIT. All other land and whole-building street numbers remain masked.

Sixty-one caveats were released across the two dates. One is excluded here: a caveat at a Changi Business Park property, treated as a re-lodgement of a transaction already recorded earlier this year rather than a second sale, and therefore excluded from the count, the value total and the matched pairs.

Caveat lodgement lags the underlying transaction. All sixty carry July caveat dates, the earliest the 6th of July.

Profit and loss is derived by matching each sale to the most recent prior transaction at the same address, computed on transacted price before costs, taxes and financing. Where the paired areas differ by more than five per cent the comparison is not like-for-like and is flagged as an area caveat rather than reported as a clean pair — one pair in this batch carries that flag.

Where a lease was renewed or topped up between the two transactions, the premium paid for the fresh tenure is not recorded in caveat data. Any gain is therefore gross of that cost and any loss understated against it. Two pairs in this batch carry a lease renewal between legs, and both are noted where they occur.

This is descriptive market commentary, not investment, valuation or transactional advice.
