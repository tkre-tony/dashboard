Centurion Accommodation REIT's DPU of 3.499 Cents Beat the Prospectus Forecast by 9.6%. Finance Costs Running 18.8% Under Forecast Supplied 46% of the Variance, and Thirteen Assets Carrying S$1.77 Billion Moved S$19,000 Across Six Months

CAREIT's DPU of 3.499 cents beat the prospectus forecast by 9.6% while profit came in 31.6% below it. Finance costs supplied 46% of the variance, and the portfolio uplift is all purchases.

By PropertyAtlas·5 August 2026·11 min read
Photo: Westlite Ubi, Singapore. Construction has commenced on an additional six-storey block of 540 beds, expected operational by the fourth quarter of 2027 and lifting total capacity to 2,190 beds. Ubi is the only one of Centurion Accommodation REIT's five Singapore properties whose carrying value moved materially over the half, and it moved because of a S$13.9 million land premium rather than any revaluation of the asset.

Centurion Accommodation REIT (CAREIT) reported a distribution per unit of 3.499 cents for the half year ended 30 June 2026, 9.6% above the prospectus forecast of 3.192 cents. Over the same six months, profit for the period came in 31.6% below forecast. Both figures are correct. The distance between them, and what closes it, is this result.

Financial Headlines

Gross revenue S$108.944 million against a forecast S$103.688 million, ahead by 5.1%. Net property income S$78.402 million against S$75.164 million, ahead by 4.3%. Finance costs S$12.097 million against S$14.892 million, 18.8% below forecast. Amount available for distribution to unitholders S$60.487 million against S$55.188 million, ahead by 9.6%. DPU 3.499 cents against 3.192 cents. Profit for the period S$34.977 million against S$51.151 million, behind by 31.6%. Aggregate leverage 29.9%, from 31.0% at 31 March. Weighted average financing cost 3.60% excluding amortisation of upfront fees, 3.84% including it. Interest coverage 5.91 times, from 6.02 times. NAV per unit S$0.87, unchanged. Portfolio 15 assets across Singapore, the United Kingdom and Australia, carried at S$2.206 billion. Ex-date 13 August, record date 14 August, payment 17 September 2026. There are no year-on-year comparatives: CAREIT listed on 25 September 2025 and was a dormant private trust before that, so the prospectus forecast is the only benchmark available.

The Beat and the Miss Are the Same Number

Profit fell short of forecast because of a S$19.126 million fair value loss on investment properties that the forecast did not carry. The note explaining it is specific: the loss is attributable mainly to stamp duty incurred on the acquisition of EPIISOD Macquarie Park, together with straight-lining adjustments recognised under SFRS(I) 16.

Those two items are quantified elsewhere in the same statements. Acquisition costs capitalised into investment properties during the half were S$16.297 million. Straight-lining adjustments added S$2.201 million. Together S$18.498 million, or 96.7% of the S$19.126 million charge. The fair value line is, in substance, the writing-off of costs capitalised three lines above it in the same reconciliation.

The charge is not tax-deductible and does not touch distributable income, which is why DPU is unaffected. It is added straight back in the distribution statement, where net tax and other adjustments run S$25.510 million against a forecast S$4.037 million. The accounting is orthodox. The consequence is that the revaluation line in this result carries almost no information about what the properties are worth.

A Portfolio That Was Not Revalued

The results presentation leads with portfolio valuation of S$2.21 billion, up 0.91% on the first quarter and up S$321.7 million over the half. No external valuation was performed at 30 June. The group carried out an internal assessment in consultation with the valuers who signed the 31 December 2025 numbers, and concluded that the key valuation inputs were substantially unchanged.

The asset-level table bears that out. Of the S$321.728 million increase in the carrying value of investment properties, S$301.670 million is the purchase of EPIISOD Macquarie Park, S$16.297 million is acquisition costs and S$13.900 million is the land premium paid for Westlite Ubi. Those three items total S$331.867 million, or 103.2% of the entire increase. Everything else — capital expenditure, straight-lining, currency translation and the fair value charge — nets to negative S$10.139 million.

Set aside the one purchase and the one land premium, and thirteen assets carrying S$1.774 billion moved by S$19,000 net across six months. Westlite Mandai, the largest single line in the book at S$544.0 million, moved by S$14,000. Westlite Toh Guan moved by S$39,000. Westlite Juniper moved by S$21,000. Singapore’s four unchanged assets rose S$513,000 in total, or 0.04%. The eight United Kingdom assets fell S$2.381 million, and Dwell East End Adelaide rose S$1.887 million — movements consistent with spot currency between the two dates rather than with any reassessment of the buildings.

Where the Beat Came From

Revenue beat forecast by S$5.256 million. By geography, and against forecast figures the manager discloses to one decimal place, Singapore revenue ran about S$3.6 million ahead, the United Kingdom about S$1.1 million and Australia about S$0.6 million. Within the Singapore figure, S$1.1 million is additional revenue from Toh Guan Expanded Capacity and Mandai Expanded Capacity — 664 retained beds that were never in the prospectus forecast at all, and a Mandai licence that arrived in May rather than the assumed July. That is 31% of the Singapore beat and 21% of the total, earned on capacity the forecast did not contemplate. The remainder is higher rental rates, more beds sold, and a stronger pound and Australian dollar against the rates assumed.

Below the property line the picture shifts. Net property income beat by S$3.238 million; finance costs came in S$2.795 million under forecast on lower drawdown and lower benchmark rates. Finance costs alone supply 46% of the gross positive variance. Against that, the manager’s performance fee of S$1.330 million appears where the forecast carried none, base fees run S$0.480 million higher, and other trust expenses S$0.740 million higher on legal and professional costs. The performance fee is assessed annually and payable only where actual DPU exceeds forecast DPU, so it exists precisely because the result beat.

Westlite Mandai Is 77.1% Full

PBWA financial occupancy was 92.2% for the half against a forecast 93.6%, a shortfall of 1.4 percentage points. The quarterly path matters more than the half-year average: 94.0% in the first quarter against a forecast 93.1%, then 90.6% in the second against a forecast 94.1%. The first quarter beat. The entire miss is in the second.

It is concentrated in one asset. Westlite Toh Guan finished its ramp at 97.2% financial occupancy, with committed occupancy of 99.0% at 31 July. Westlite Woodlands stood at 100.0% even while absorbing marine-sector non-renewals tied to the impending relocation of Sembawang Shipyard. Westlite Mandai — 9,986 beds, the largest asset in the portfolio by value — recorded financial occupancy of 77.1%, with committed occupancy of 87.2% at 31 July.

The manager attributes this to the phased receipt of licences and the mechanical dilution of adding 4,360 beds mid-period, which is a fair description. Tenant retention nonetheless fell to 74.6% for the half from 79.4% in the first quarter, and part of that is tenants moving from existing blocks into the new ones — a transfer within the portfolio rather than a loss from it. Revenue still beat forecast, which means rate did the work that occupancy did not. That is a serviceable outcome for one half and a thinner one to repeat.

The Master Lease Steps Up 42%, Then Ends

CAREIT completed the A$345.0 million acquisition of EPIISOD Macquarie Park on 13 January 2026, a 732-bed asset in Sydney. The vendor is 25% owned by Centurion Corporation Limited, the sponsor, and 75% by Centurion Properties Pte. Ltd. The asset is master-leased back to Herring Road Management Pty Ltd until 31 December 2027 — a company with the same 25/75 ownership. Both sides of the transaction sit inside the sponsor group.

The master lease provides fixed rent of A$14.1 million for FY2026 and A$20.0 million for FY2027. On the A$345.0 million cost that is roughly 4.2% annualised in the first year and 5.8% in the second, a step-up of 41.8%. From 1 January 2028 the asset stands on its own leasing after ramping across two academic years. The single largest tenant contributes 6.87% of gross rental income for the half; the next largest contributes 0.94%. Excluding the master tenant, no tenant exceeds 1%.

The statements record that CAREIT has not obtained a general mandate from unitholders for interested person transactions. Nothing here is irregular, and the fixed rent does what the manager says it does, which is supply income visibility during a ramp. But 6.87% of rental income currently rests on a related-party lease that reprices upward once and then expires, and the ramp underneath it has not yet been tested by the market.

The Two Segments Are Diverging

Singapore worker accommodation produced S$77.266 million of revenue and S$56.488 million of net property income, a margin of 73.1%, against finance costs of S$5.009 million. Student accommodation in the United Kingdom and Australia produced S$31.678 million of revenue and S$21.914 million of net property income, a margin of 69.2%, against finance costs of S$7.088 million. Student accommodation carries 32.3% of its net property income in interest against 8.9% for the worker portfolio, and after the fair value charge lands in that segment it reports a pre-tax loss of S$4.863 million.

Singapore represents 64.0% of portfolio value and 72.0% of net property income. The student portfolio performed well on its own terms — 98.8% financial occupancy against a forecast 96.9%, 99.1% in the United Kingdom and 98.0% in Australia. But the earnings engine is unambiguously Singapore, and the geographic diversification is presently costing more in financing than it is contributing in margin.

Capacity Peaks in 2028

Portfolio capacity has grown 25.7% since listing, from 24,054 beds to 30,236 at 30 June 2026. The presentation carries the year-by-year path forward, and it does not continue upward. Capacity reaches 30,781 beds in 2027 and holds there through 2028, then falls to 29,511 in 2029 as the 664 retained beds under Toh Guan Expanded Capacity expire on 31 December 2028 and 606 beds are demolished for the Toh Guan Phase 2 development.

Beyond the chart’s horizon sits a larger step. The 1,980 retained beds under Mandai Expanded Capacity run only to 31 December 2030. The Westlite Ubi development adds 540 beds by the fourth quarter of 2027 and Dwell Hotwells House loses five on completion of its ensuite conversion. Both retained-capacity blocks are, by construction, temporary; they are counted in today’s 30,236 and they have expiry dates attached.

The Sponsor Is Building the Competition

On the same morning these results were released, Centurion Corporation Limited announced it had been awarded the tender for a Kranji Close site for the development of around 7,000 beds. It is also the highest bidder for a 30-year Lok Yang Way site of around 5,000 beds, and acquired a 65% stake in a freehold site at 7 Kim Chuan Lane in February. The sponsor’s disclosed portfolio pipeline runs to about 9,198 beds across Singapore, the United Kingdom and Australia, over which CAREIT holds a right of first refusal.

That pipeline is the growth channel and the competition at once. Five PBWA land sites totalling 40,200 beds are scheduled for tender across 2026 and 2027, of which 25,200 beds across four sites had been launched by 31 July. Against CAREIT’s entire Singapore book of 26,742 beds, the tender programme alone is roughly one and a half times the portfolio. Offsetting it, approximately 102,800 beds of alternative worker accommodation have leases expiring between 2026 and 2030, and upgrading under the Dormitory Transition Scheme will withdraw capacity during the transition. Knight Frank and the Dormitory Association of Singapore forecast bed rents rising around 5% in 2026.

Demand has been the reliable side of this equation and is showing its first deceleration. Work permit holders in the construction, marine shipyard and process sectors rose 5.6% to 482,600 at December 2025. The construction sector grew 6.2% year on year in the second quarter of 2026, against 12.9% in the first. The Building and Construction Authority forecasts S$47 billion to S$53 billion of contracts awarded in 2026, then S$39 billion to S$46 billion annually from 2027 to 2030 — a step down from the current year at the same point new supply arrives.

On compliance, Westlite Ubi and the new blocks at Toh Guan and Mandai are described as fully compliant with the New Dormitory Standards. The remaining assets are described as complying with key specifications. Interim standards apply by 2030 and the full standards by 2040. Total contracted capital commitments at 30 June stood at S$19.0 million. What the older stock will cost to bring to full compliance is not quantified anywhere in this pack.

What to Watch

Leverage at 29.9% with S$380.0 million of headroom to the 40% threshold, no debt maturing until FY2028, a hedge ratio lifted to 71.6% from 56.2% and a financing cost 48 basis points inside forecast constitute a genuinely strong balance sheet, and the FTSE EPRA Nareit Global Developed Index inclusion on 22 June, within nine months of listing, is a real milestone. Interest coverage of 5.91 times is comfortable, though it has eased from 6.02 times and current liabilities exceeded current assets by S$37.2 million at the balance date, covered by S$160.6 million of undrawn committed facilities.

Three things to carry into the second half. Whether Mandai converts 87.2% committed into financial occupancy, since that is where the forecast shortfall lives and rate cannot substitute indefinitely. Whether the finance cost advantage persists once the benchmark tailwind is in the base, given it supplied 46% of this half’s gross variance. And what happens at EPIISOD Macquarie Park from January 2028, when a related-party master lease that has just stepped up 42% gives way to open-market leasing. The manager expects gross revenue to exceed the prospectus forecast again in the second half, helped by around S$2.9 million more from the two expanded-capacity blocks. The distribution policy pays out 100% of distributable income only until 31 December 2027, and at least 90% thereafter.
