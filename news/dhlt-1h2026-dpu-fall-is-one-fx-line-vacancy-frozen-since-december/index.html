Daiwa House Logistics Trust's DPU Fell 18.8% to 1.82 Cents and the Entire 0.42-Cent Decline Matches One Unrealised Currency Adjustment. Occupancy Has Not Moved Since December 2025, and Nor Have the Three Properties Holding It

Daiwa House Logistics Trust's DPU fell 18.8% to 1.82 cents, but the entire 0.42-cent decline matches one non-cash currency adjustment. Occupancy has not moved since December 2025.

By PropertyAtlas·5 August 2026·9 min read
Photo: D Project Nagano Suzaka S in Nagano Prefecture — one of sixteen Daiwa House Logistics Trust properties at full occupancy as at 30 June 2026. All of the trust's vacancy sits in three other buildings, and 70.5% of it in one. Credit: Daiwa House Logistics Trust.

Daiwa House Logistics Trust (DHLT) reported a distribution per unit of 1.82 cents for the half year ended 30 June 2026, down 18.8%. Distributable income fell S$2.904 million. Total return attributable to unitholders over the same half rose S$651,000. The whole of that gap sits in one non-cash line, and portfolio occupancy has not moved since December.

Financial Headlines

Gross revenue S$25.730 million, down 11.8%. Net property income S$19.512 million, down 13.4%. Distributable income to unitholders S$12.792 million, down 18.5%. DPU 1.82 cents, down 18.8%, wholly tax-exempt income. Total return attributable to unitholders S$10.318 million, up 6.7%. Portfolio occupancy 87.8%. WALE by gross rental income 6.1 years. Aggregate leverage 40.1%. Interest coverage 4.8 times. Weighted average all-in borrowing cost 2.05%, 99.3% fixed. Portfolio valuation S$811.085 million. NAV per unit S$0.63. Ex-date 13 August, record date 14 August, payment 28 September 2026.

The DPU fell 0.42 cents and one non-cash line swung 0.42 cents

Distributable income to unitholders fell from S$15.696 million to S$12.792 million. Total return attributable to unitholders over the same half rose, from S$9.667 million to S$10.318 million. The entire difference is in the distribution adjustments line, which fell from S$6.029 million to S$2.474 million.

In the prior half the trust added back an unrealised foreign exchange loss of S$2.038 million. In this half it deducted an unrealised foreign exchange gain of S$913,000. The swing between the two is S$2.951 million. Against a weighted average 700,825,446 units in issue that is 0.421 cents per unit. The DPU fell 0.42 cents.

Net property income fell S$3.007 million over the same half and does not appear in that bridge at all. It was absorbed one line higher. Other income moved from a S$1.517 million charge to a S$1.264 million credit, a S$2.781 million swing driven by the same currency movement, and the fair value charge on investment properties was S$597,000 smaller. The currency gain that rescued the profit line is then stripped out of the distribution. The operating decline is left standing on its own.

The reported DPU decline is, arithmetically, a non-cash accounting swing. The operating decline is real, and it is somewhere else.

A charge the distribution statement does not add back

DHLT's distribution adjustments comprise five items: the manager's fee paid in units, the fair value change in derivatives, unrealised foreign exchange, amortisation of loan and bond upfront fees, and deferred tax. The fair value movement on investment properties is not among them.

That movement was a S$1.133 million charge this half, arising almost entirely from right-of-use assets and assets corresponding to asset-retirement obligations on the leasehold land. It is a non-cash item, it flows through total return, and it is carried straight into distributable income without an add-back. At 701,587,236 units it is worth 0.16 cents, or roughly 9% of the distribution. The prior half carried the same treatment on a S$1.730 million charge, so this is policy rather than a change — and a conservative one. It is also not a line most S-REIT distribution statements leave in.

Occupancy has not moved since December, and neither have the three properties

The press release describes portfolio occupancy as having remained stable at 87.8%. It is stable. It is also the third consecutive reporting date at exactly that figure.

The three property-level rates were not restated in the first-quarter business update, but no leases expired in that quarter and the portfolio rate was unchanged, so nothing could have moved. At the two dates that do report them the figures are identical. Seven months, three problem buildings, not one basis point.

Portfolio occupancy was 97.6% at the FY2024 close. Two leases were renewed in this half, at an average uplift of 5.6%. In FY2025 nine leases were renewed or newly let, all at higher rents, at a weighted average of 11.1%. Volume fell from nine to two and the spread roughly halved.

The improvement the Manager points to happened after the balance sheet date. A vacant unit at DPL Koriyama commenced lease in July 2026, lifting that property to 92.3%; in the same month a lease that expired on 30 June vacated. Net of both, portfolio occupancy as at 31 July 2026 was 88.5%.

The occupancy rate is weighted by net lettable area, and it reconciles to the property schedule exactly.

Sixteen of nineteen properties are full. DPL Sendai Port alone is 8.6% of portfolio net lettable area and carries S$107.346 million, or 13.2% of the S$811.085 million portfolio, making it the second largest asset after DPL Kawasaki Yako. It is 68.1% empty, and has been for at least seven months. Resolving that single building would lift portfolio occupancy above 96%. This vacancy split is a PropertyAtlas derivation from the trust's own property schedule; the trust does not publish it.

The release describes a maintained long WALE of 6.1 years and a healthy interest coverage ratio of 4.8 times. Both statements are accurate. Both metrics are also on their third consecutive decline. Interest cover was 6.6 times a year ago.

The WALE is held up at the ends of the distribution rather than through the middle. The single Vietnam asset carries 17.3 years; the Japan portfolio is 5.5. Six Japan properties sit under two years — DPL Okayama Hayashima 2 at 0.5, DPL Ibaraki Yuki at 0.6, DPL Okayama Hayashima at 1.4, DPL Koriyama at 1.7, DPL Sapporo Higashi Kariki at 1.8 and DPL Iwakuni 1 & 2 at 1.9 — and every one of them is fully occupied today. By gross rental income, 11.4% expires in the remainder of 2026 and 18.8% in 2027.

Concentration sits behind that. Mitsubishi Shokuhin is 22.9% of gross rental income on its own, the top ten tenants are 65.5%, and third-party logistics operators are 79.6% of income.

Every dollar of the decline is translation. No independent valuation was taken at the half year. Note 13 states that carrying values at 30 June 2026 were internally assessed by the Manager, in consultation with the external valuers conducting the annual valuation, and that fair value approximates the 31 December 2025 carrying value adjusted for capital expenditure. The valuation inputs are carried forward unchanged: the Japan discount rate range of 3.15% to 4.70% and terminal capitalisation range of 3.25% to 5.00% are disclosed as applying at both 30 June 2026 and 31 December 2025.

DPL Sendai Port therefore carries JPY 13.5 billion on a valuation struck at 31 December 2025, and has not been independently retested at 31.9% occupancy. The next independent valuation falls at 31 December 2026.

The translation reserve has taken a third of unitholders' funds

NAV per unit fell from S$0.65 to S$0.63. Gross unitholders' funds barely moved, from S$663.514 million to S$659.600 million, a decline of 0.6%. The foreign currency translation reserve widened from negative S$206.225 million to negative S$220.727 million, a S$14.502 million move in six months.

That reserve now stands at 33.5% of gross unitholders' funds. The Manager states plainly in the results presentation that JPY has weakened against SGD by more than 30% since DHLT listed in November 2021. The exchange rate applied moved from S$1.00 = JPY 122.00 at the year end to S$1.00 = JPY 125.76 at 30 June.

The same distortion runs through the income statement. Japan net property income fell 4.6% in JPY terms and 13.8% in Singapore dollars, so roughly two-thirds of the reported decline is translation rather than operations. The Vietnam asset was marginally lower in VND on higher operating expenses, but on a cash basis it was 2.7% higher on built-in escalation. It is 3.2% of portfolio value and 5.8% of net property income, at a 96.0% NPI margin against Japan's 74.9%.

All of it was drawn or issued while Japanese policy rates were negative. The Bank of Japan took its benchmark to 1% in June 2026, held in July, and signalled that it will continue raising. The Manager states directly that it expects these borrowings to refinance at higher rates. The trust's all-in weighted average borrowing cost is 2.05% and 99.3% of debt is fixed — protection that expires at maturity rather than before it.

The disclosed sensitivity is steep. A 100 basis point increase in the weighted average interest rate takes interest coverage from 4.8 times to 3.2 times. Excluding perpetual securities distributions the ratio would be 5.3 times, so the perpetuals already absorb half a turn at a coupon of roughly 2%.

Two further framings sit inside the same disclosure. Aggregate leverage of 40.1% excludes lease liabilities arising from land rent; those total S$125.875 million, and finance costs on them were S$1.290 million of the S$4.925 million total finance expense. And a footnote in the presentation gives total debt including perpetual securities against net asset value at 80.4%. The trust also reports current liabilities exceeding current assets by S$39.1 million at 30 June, with the Manager stating it expects to refinance and meet obligations as they fall due.

One phrase that was not there before

Across the FY2025 results, the 1Q FY2026 business update and this release, this is the first occasion on which the Manager has paired DPL Sendai Port with asset recycling. It appears three times in a single set of documents: in the outlook commentary of the financial statements, in the chief executive's quote, and in the results presentation. The wording is consistent throughout — the Manager seeks to improve the occupancy of DPL Sendai Port and is concurrently exploring various options regarding the property, including asset recycling.

For an asset that is 13.2% of portfolio value, carried on a seven-month-old independent valuation, and 68.1% vacant for at least that long, the introduction of that phrase is the most consequential change in the pack. It is not in the headline of the press release.

The distribution's composition improved as its size fell

This half's 1.82 cents is wholly a tax-exempt income distribution. The preceding distribution, 2.09 cents for the second half of FY2025, was 1.02 cents of tax-exempt income and 1.07 cents of capital — 51.2% of it a return of capital. On the income component alone, the sequential move is from 1.02 cents to 1.82 cents.

Earnings per unit moved in the opposite direction to the DPU, rising from 1.44 cents to 1.52 cents. The distribution still exceeds earnings, funded by the deferred tax and financing-fee add-backs, but the quality of what sits underneath it is better than the headline suggests.

What to watch

Five leases expire in the second half, about 10% of net lettable area, with tenants for four of them — roughly 9% of NLA — having indicated an intention to renew. Excluding the lease that expired on 30 June, the remainder is 10.8% by gross rental income.

Whether DPL Sendai Port is actually marketed. The phrase is on the record now, and the asset is large enough that a disposal would reset both the occupancy rate and the leverage ratio in one step.

The 31 December 2026 independent valuation, which will be the first external mark on Sendai Port since the vacancy became structural.

The November refinancing and the perpetual securities reset, together 27.6% of borrowings plus S$35.75 million of hybrid, against a policy rate that has moved from negative to 1%.

And whether the July improvement holds. Portfolio occupancy at 31 July was 88.5%. It has taken seven months to move seven-tenths of a point.
