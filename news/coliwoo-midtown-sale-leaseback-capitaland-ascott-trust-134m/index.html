Coliwoo to Sell Midtown to CapitaLand Ascott Trust for S$134.0m, Five Months After It Opened

The price is 3.1% above the only disclosed independent valuation. Of the S$14.1m the asset contributed in the first half, S$14.0m was fair value.

By PropertyAtlas·8 August 2026·8 min read
Photo: Coliwoo Midtown at 141 Middle Road. The 212-room co-living property opened in March 2026 and is being sold to CapitaLand Ascott Trust for S$134.0 million five months later. Credit: Coliwoo Holdings Limited.

Coliwoo Holdings Limited will sell Coliwoo Midtown at 141 Middle Road to CapitaLand Ascott Trust for S$134.0 million and lease it straight back for ten years. The 212-room co-living property opened in March 2026. It is being sold five months later.

The vendor is Coliwoo (TK) Pte. Ltd., 80 per cent owned by the company. The purchaser is DBS Trustee Limited as trustee of CLAS Uptown Trust, a vehicle constituted for this transaction and wholly owned by CapitaLand Ascott Trust. The two sides entered into a put and call option agreement on 6 August 2026.

The price against the valuation

Savills Valuation and Professional Services (S) Pte. Ltd. assessed the market value at S$130.0 million as at 1 August 2026. The agreed price is S$134.0 million excluding goods and services tax, for the property together with plant and equipment.

Net proceeds of approximately S$41.0 million are 30.6 per cent of the headline price; the balance goes to transaction expenses and to repaying borrowings secured on the asset.

The leasehold appears in the buyer’s release, not the seller’s

Coliwoo’s announcement states the tenure plainly. The property comprises the whole of land lot 332W of Town Subdivision 11, on a leasehold of 99 years commencing 2 May 1978. That term expires on 1 May 2077, leaving approximately 50.7 years unexpired at the option date and approximately 40.6 years at the end of the ten-year leaseback.

CapitaLand Ascott Trust’s release goes further. It puts the remaining leasehold at 51 years twice, and identifies an opportunity to top up the remaining term to a fresh 99-year leasehold, subject to authorities’ approval, as something that supports the long-term valuation of the asset. Serena Teo, chief executive of the trust’s managers, names the top-up in her quoted remarks.

Coliwoo’s press release, issued the same morning, does not mention the tenure at all, and neither Coliwoo document refers to a lease top-up. Both companies disclosed what was required of them. The emphasis differs.

Almost all of the asset’s half-year profit was revaluation

The relative figures under Rule 1006 of the Listing Manual, computed off the unaudited accounts for the six months to 31 March 2026, are unusually high. All three applicable tests clear 20 per cent, which is what makes this a major transaction under Rule 1014 and sends it to an extraordinary general meeting.

The company’s own footnote to the second test is the line worth reading twice. Net profit attributable to the asset for the six months from 1 October 2025 to 31 March 2026 was S$14.1 million — of which S$14.0 million was fair value recognised.

That is 99.3 per cent of the asset’s half-year contribution. Coliwoo Midtown opened in March 2026, in the closing weeks of the period, and its operating contribution across the half was close to nil. The 80.2 per cent figure that carries this transaction over the major-transaction threshold is, in substance, a revaluation.

The revaluation is visible in the parent’s audited accounts. LHN Limited’s annual report for the year ended 30 September 2025 carries 141 Middle Road at S$102.5 million, against S$86.0 million a year earlier. By 31 March 2026 the book value was S$124.5 million. The S$22.0 million step across that half comprises the S$14.0 million fair value gain and roughly S$8.0 million of capitalised expenditure.

One announcement, two balance sheet dates

The Rule 1006 relative figures are computed off 31 March 2026. The financial-effects tables in the same announcement are computed off the audited accounts to 30 September 2025, and on 312,500,000 shares — the pre-listing count.

Coliwoo listed on 6 November 2025 and has had 480,800,000 shares since. The announcement uses that larger count in the same document, for the Rule 1006(c) market capitalisation of S$248.8 million.

So the pro forma figures after the sale — earnings per share of 10.46 cents, net tangible assets per share of 45.81 cents — rest on a share count the company has not had for nine months, and on an equity base that predates the roughly S$101 million raised at listing. The company sets out the basis in its notes and flags the pre-listing share count explicitly. It is disclosed, not concealed. But the two halves of one announcement do not stand on the same footing, and the net asset value per share Coliwoo reported at 31 March 2026 — 47.32 cents — was already above the pro forma figure of 45.81 cents.

What still has to happen

Completion is conditional. The conditions precedent include shareholder approval at an extraordinary general meeting yet to be convened; approval from the Minister for Law under section 25 of the Residential Property Act 1976; remission of Additional Buyer’s Stamp Duty and of the residential and non-residential Buyer’s Stamp Duty differential; remission of Seller’s Stamp Duty; completion of repair and rectification works on certain defects; and — for a building that has been trading since March and was close to 90 per cent occupied in July — the vendor obtaining the Certificate of Statutory Completion.

Separately, the vendor is replacing the chiller system and is seeking Building and Construction Authority approval for the works. If that approval is not in hand by completion, the purchaser may withhold 1.5 per cent of the price — approximately S$2.0 million — until it is.

The two sides also describe the timing differently. Coliwoo states that the completion date falls on 30 September 2026 if the option is exercised on or before 23 September, or five business days after exercise if later, and books the estimated gross gain of approximately S$9.2 million in the financial year ended 30 September 2026. CapitaLand Ascott Trust says completion is expected in the fourth quarter of 2026. Those are compatible only if the option is exercised after 23 September — in which case the gain falls into Coliwoo’s next financial year instead.

A clarificatory announcement issued the same day corrected the exercise threshold in the original clause from 30 September to 23 September, which is what gives the five-business-day window its room to run.

What the seller keeps

All 212 rooms stay in Coliwoo’s portfolio, moving from the owned segment to the leased segment. The master lessee is Coliwoo Midtown Pte. Ltd. — not the vendor. The lease runs ten years with an option to renew for a further ten by mutual agreement, on fixed rent with annual indexation only from the second anniversary of commencement.

CapitaLand Ascott Trust is acquiring on a 4.1 per cent EBITDA yield on agreed property value, against the 2.3 per cent exit yield on The Robertson House by The Crest Collection, which it divested on 30 July 2026 and whose proceeds fund this purchase. At 4.1 per cent on S$134.0 million the implied annual triple-net rent is approximately S$5.49 million — about S$2,160 per room per month, or S$7.28 psf of gross floor area per month.

One figure appears in neither release. Coliwoo owns 80 per cent of the vendor. On the estimated S$9.2 million gross gain, approximately S$1.84 million accrues to the non-controlling shareholder and approximately S$7.36 million to Coliwoo shareholders.

The recycling arc

Midtown is the third disposal in eight months. Coliwoo completed the sale and leaseback of Coliwoo Hotel Pasir Panjang in January 2026, launched a portfolio sale of seven freehold living assets at an indicative S$218.5 million in March 2026, and has now agreed Midtown. The group held 3,568 rooms at 31 March 2026 and is targeting close to 4,000 in Singapore by the end of 2026 and 10,000 by 2030.

Kelvin Lim Lung Tieng, executive chairman and chief executive of Coliwoo, framed the transaction as capital recycling — acquisition, renovation, operation, disposal and leaseback — that unlocks capital while maintaining operational continuity.

The extraordinary general meeting has not been convened and the circular has not been dispatched.
