Stoneweg Europe Stapled Trust Will Pay Its Sponsor €114.0 Million To Internalise Its Managers And Platform — The Fees It Gives Up And The Cost Of Running The Platform Itself Are €2.6 Million Apart, Third-Party Mandates Supply 91 Per Cent Of The €10.1 Million Uplift, NTA Per Security Falls 6.0 Per Cent And The Sponsor’s Stake Rises To 33.03 Per Cent

Stoneweg Europe Stapled Trust will buy the REIT Manager, the BT Trustee-Manager and the pan-European platform from SWI Group for €114.0 million, €80.0 million of it funded by new stapled securities the sponsor subscribes at €2.00. The fees SERT stops…

By PropertyAtlas·28 September 2026·12 min read
PropertyAtlas figure card. Stoneweg Europe Stapled Trust gives up €26.4 million of FY2025 sponsor fees and takes on an estimated €23.8 million to run the platform in-house, a €2.6 million difference; third-party mandates supply €9.2 million after tax of the €10.1 million pro forma uplift. Credit: PropertyAtlas analysis of the SERT announcement dated 28th September 2026.

Stoneweg Europe Stapled Trust will buy its REIT Manager, its BT Trustee-Manager and the European platform that runs its properties from its sponsor, SWI Group, for €114.0 million, most of it funded by 40.0 million new stapled securities the sponsor subscribes at €2.00. The managers say the deal lifts FY2025 pro forma distribution per stapled security by 5.9 per cent. The announcement’s own bridge shows where that comes from: the €26.4 million of fees SERT stops paying and the €23.8 million it expects to spend running the platform itself are €2.6 million apart, and most of the €10.1 million uplift comes from third-party management contracts that still need their owners’ consent to transfer.

What Is Being Bought, And At What Price

On 28th September 2026 the managers announced a conditional share purchase agreement with five SWI Group entities. SERT would acquire the shares of the REIT Manager and the BT Trustee-Manager; the European logistics and light industrial asset and property management platform, with selected third-party management contracts; and minority co-investment stakes in the assets those contracts cover. The managers would be held through a new SERT Share Trust, with stapled securityholders as its beneficiaries and British and Malayan Trustees Limited as trustee. The co-investment stakes go into the Business Trust.

The platform employs about 100 people: roughly 85 across ten European jurisdictions, 10 full-time staff in Singapore and five non-executive directors (section 3.3.1, page 18 of the announcement). It manages SERT’s 96 assets and 1.6 million square metres, and 46 third-party assets of about 618,000 square metres under 11 mandates (section 1.1, page 3).

Adding €5.1 million of stamp duty, restructuring and professional costs gives a total acquisition cost of €127.5 million, about S$187.4 million (section 2.8, page 13). Against the €141.2 million midpoint of BDO’s range, that all-in cost leaves a margin of €13.7 million, rather than the €19.2 million of “day-one value creation” in the media release, which compares the platform price alone with the platform valuation. Measured against earnings, €114.0 million is 8.0 times the €14.324 million of net profits the announcement attributes to the entities acquired for Rule 1006(b) purposes (section 7, page 35).

Funding is €80.0 million from the sponsor’s subscription and up to €34.0 million in cash, plus up to €8.4 million in cash for the co-investments (section 2.9, page 13). If none of the third-party consents arrive in time, the consideration falls by €29.1 million to €93.3 million, €21.3 million of that reduction relating to the third-party contracts (section 2.6, page 11).

Where The €10.1 Million Comes From

PropertyAtlas argued on 17th August that internalisation would relieve SERT’s fee load rather than add to it. Fees to sponsor-related entities were €13.5 million in the first half alone, 36.7 per cent of the income available for distribution. This announcement gives the first measure of how much relief there is. The pro forma bridge is in section 6.1 on page 32:

The first two lines are the internalisation in its narrow sense. SERT stops paying €26.4 million and starts paying €23.8 million to run the same platform itself. The difference is €2.6 million.

The third-party mandates contribute €11.0 million of revenue, or €9.2 million after the tax the bridge deducts. That is 91 per cent of the €10.1 million total. The lines as printed sum to €10.0 million; the stated €10.1 million is consistent with rounding and with the €10.06 million increase in distributable income in section 6.2.

Two qualifications apply. The €23.8 million runs the whole platform, including the teams that serve the third-party mandates, so it is not purely the cost of managing SERT. And the bridge sets FY2025 fees against FY2027 cost and revenue estimates, so the years on either side are not the same.

The mandates transfer only with “Required Third-Party Consents” from the investors whose assets are managed. The 5.9 per cent in the media release’s first line assumes every one is obtained.

The no-consent column still shows €7.8 million of additional distributable income. The bridge, with the mandate revenue removed, does not reach that figure, and the announcement does not show the bridge for that scenario. The circular should. The “approximately €28 million” of FY2025 fees cited in section 3.1.2 on page 14 is also €1.6 million more than the €26.4 million given up in the bridge, and the two are not reconciled.

The announcement describes NAV as stable. Before the transaction NAV and NTA are the same number; afterwards they differ by €66.6 million, which is the intangible value of the platform and its contracts. On the disclosed absolutes, NTA per security falls 6.0 per cent, against 5.9 per cent DPS accretion. Without the third-party consents it is €1.90. The €7.8 million difference in NTA between the two scenarios matches the €7.8 million price reduction on the co-investment stakes in section 2.6: the stakes are the tangible part of what is being bought.

Gearing barely moves. On the EMTN definition, net gearing goes from 41.9 per cent to 41.8 per cent with all consents and 41.1 per cent without, because the €80.0 million of new equity offsets €47.5 million or €18.4 million of new debt (section 6.4, pages 33–34).

The €2.00 Securities And The Sponsor’s Stake

The media release presents the €2.00 issue price as a 33 per cent premium to a volume-weighted average price of about €1.50 on 24th September 2026. The announcement’s own Rule 1006(c) calculation uses a VWAP of €1.4640 on 25th September (section 7, page 35); against that the premium is 36.6 per cent.

That premium is measured against a market price of 0.72 times NAV per security of €2.03 at 31st December 2025. Against book, €2.00 is 1.5 per cent below NAV per security and 2.0 per cent above the adjusted NAV of €1.96: the subscription is struck at close to book value. The €20.0 million gap between the €114.0 million purchase price and the €94.0 million “effective consideration” is that premium, 40.0 million securities at €0.50 each (section 1.1, page 2). The sponsor receives €114.0 million for the platform and puts €80.0 million of it back into SERT at €2.00. The new securities carry no entitlement to accrued distributions at issue.

The holding crosses the 30 per cent level at which the Take-over Code would ordinarily require a mandatory general offer. The Securities Industry Council has granted a whitewash waiver, subject to conditions that include a whitewash resolution by independent securityholders (section 9, page 39). The sponsor has committed to a 12-month lock-up (section 3.3.3, page 22).

What Stays With The Sponsor, And What It Costs

Internalisation removes the base, property management and acquisition fees SERT pays its sponsor. Two new agreements with SWI Group entities create fees of a different kind, with caps set out in Appendix A on pages 45–47.

The exclusivity agreement with SWI Capital Holding (SWICH) bars the SWI Group from logistics and light industrial assets in the EU, the UK, Switzerland and five other European states other than through SERT. SWICH must pass on off-market opportunities it comes across, but is not obliged to look for them (section 5.2, page 25). SERT pays a finder’s fee of up to 1 per cent of the gross acquisition price on opportunities SWICH introduces, and up to 2 per cent of equity deployed by capital partners it introduces. The announcement’s own illustration of what external management costs, in section 3.2.2 on page 17, assumes a 1.0 per cent acquisition fee paid away to the manager. On a deal SWICH introduces, the cap on the finder’s fee is the same 1 per cent.

The data centre agreement with SWI Digital Investments is non-exclusive, runs for five years and renews for two-year periods (section 5.6, page 31). Its caps include an acquisition fee of 1.25 per cent, combined development and technical project management of up to 3.75 per cent of eligible development cost, 5.00 per cent of approved capital expenditure on existing assets, asset management of 0.45 to 0.55 per cent a year, and new-lease fees of the higher of 2.00 per cent of total lease commitment or 6 per cent of first-year gross base rent. Approval at the EGM will count as specific approval under Chapter 9 of the Listing Manual for all fees within those caps, so they will not be aggregated into SERT’s interested person transactions (section 5.5, page 31). The exclusivity lapses once SWICH’s stake falls below 5 per cent (section 5.4.7, page 30).

Three other costs sit alongside. Transitional services between SERT and SWI Group run at €45,000 a month for a limited period (section 2.10, page 13). Retention and incentive awards to key employees worth about €2.0 million in stapled securities vest over two years, subject to securityholder approval (section 2.4, page 8). And the platform workforce cost €13.5 million in FY2025, of which the eight named senior executives accounted for €2.7 million; individual remuneration is not disclosed (section 2.4, page 7).

Five Disclosure Points

None of these changes the direction of the transaction. They are listed because each is a figure a reader might otherwise take on trust.

Co-investment premium. The €8.4 million consideration is stated to be a 5.4 per cent premium to the midpoint (section 2.7, page 12). The announcement gives the midpoint as €8.0 million, the media release table gives €7.9 million (page 2), and the arithmetic midpoint of the €7.5 million to €8.4 million range is €7.95 million. The premium is 5.0, 6.3 or 5.7 per cent respectively. None produces 5.4 per cent.

Day-one value. The media release gives €19.2 million, the platform price against the platform valuation; section 3.2.3 on page 17 gives €18.8 million, total consideration against total valuation. Both tie on their own basis.

FY2025 fees. About €28 million in section 3.1.2; €26.4 million in the bridge.

Portfolio value. €2.4 billion in section 1.1 on page 1, €2.3 billion in the media release, and €2.2 billion in the “About SERT” paragraph on page 48.

Whether the subscribers are vendors. Note (3) to the Rule 1006(d) table on page 35 says the entity subscribing for the new securities “is not one of the Vendors”. Section 8.2 on page 37 says each subscribing entity “is also a Vendor”, and all three appear in the vendor list in section 2.2. The classification is unaffected either way: 40.0 million securities are 7.2 per cent of the 555.1 million in issue, below the 20 per cent major-transaction threshold.

What Happens Next

Completion requires MAS approvals, the SIC waiver conditions, securityholder approval of the internalisation and the whitewash resolution at an EGM, SGX approval-in-principle for the new securities, the third-party consents, a reorganisation of the target companies, and SERT securing funding for the cash component (section 2.2.1, pages 5–6). If everything except the consents is in place, the managers and platform can complete first and the consent-dependent pieces follow in cash. The long-stop date is 5.00 p.m. on 23rd December 2026, and completion is the later of five business days after the last condition and 29th December 2026 (sections 2.2.2 and 2.2.3, page 6).

The transaction is discloseable rather than major under Chapter 10, with relative figures of 15.5, 15.1 and 7.2 per cent (section 7, page 35). It needs securityholder approval because it is an interested person transaction worth about €202.4 million including the subscription, 16.9 per cent of the latest audited NTA of €1,195.573 million, against a 5 per cent threshold (section 8.4, page 38). SERT has already entered into about €50.0 million of interested person transactions with the SWI Group this financial year, 4.2 per cent of NTA (section 8.5, pages 38–39). Ernst & Young Corporate Finance is the independent financial adviser; its opinion and the Audit and Risk Committee’s view will be in the circular (section 11, page 43).

SERT would become the second internalised REIT on SGX, after Alpha Integrated REIT completed its internalisation in October 2025.

The Read

Internalisation removes the fees; it does not remove the cost of the people the fees paid for. On SERT’s own bridge the two are €2.6 million apart. The accretion securityholders are asked to vote on comes mainly from the third-party mandates that travel with the platform, and it is 3.1 per cent rather than 5.9 per cent if they do not. In exchange, NTA per security falls 6.0 per cent and the sponsor ends up holding a third of the trust through securities issued at close to book. The circular is where the €7.8 million no-consent uplift, and the independent financial adviser’s view of the price, need to be explained.

The margin of the all-in acquisition cost against the valuation midpoint, the price-to-profit multiple, the net fee saving, the mandates’ share of the uplift, the €66.6 million difference between NAV and NTA, the percentage fall in NTA per security, the premium against the 25th September VWAP, the price-to-NAV ratio, the issue price against NAV and adjusted NAV, the sponsor’s post-issue stake and the recomputed co-investment premiums are PropertyAtlas calculations from the announcement and media release of 28th September 2026. The post-issue stake assumes no other securities are issued and excludes the retention awards. The price-to-NAV ratio sets a September 2026 price against a 31st December 2025 NAV. Fees to sponsor-related entities in 1H 2026 are from PropertyAtlas coverage of 17th August 2026.

Update, 30th September 2026. On 29th September the managers issued a clarification restating the €10.1 million as platform earnings: €37.4 million of fee revenue, being the €26.4 million SERT pays and €11.0 million from third-party mandates, less €23.8 million of costs, gives EBIT of €13.7 million, from which €1.8 million of tax and €1.8 million of financing costs are deducted. The clarification confirms that all €11.0 million of mandate revenue is included. It does not reconcile the “approximately €28 million” of FY2025 fees with the €26.4 million in the bridge, or show the bridge for the scenario in which no third-party consents are obtained.
